FLORA PAINT / PALETTE AND LIGHTING ACCEPTANCE TARGET
=================================================
Status: reference analysis and proposed acceptance criteria, 13 November 2026.
No scene, material, lighting or gameplay changes are part of this analysis.

Decision
The current FloraTrail palette is not ready for a broad LiDAR rollout. Its
source-asset calibration is useful groundwork, but matching one tree in a
controlled rig does not establish the reference's complete appearance. The
current daylight view needs coherent material colour and selective lighting.

Reference roles
Primary daylight palette: user-supplied green woodland with the white animal
(0366285E.../3-Photo-3.jpg). This is the working daylight target, not a claim
that the user has chosen one exact time of day for every map.
Light structure: the Flora Paint forest-path image (87d6a348...). Use its
coloured shade, selective sun patches and separation between forest layers.
Open sunlight: the Flora Paint conifers at low sun (92317881...). Use the
relationship between gold-facing foliage and cool interiors, not its entire
sunset grade as a mandatory daylight colour cast.
The pink/teal forest and blue-fog birch forest demonstrate alternate palettes
and atmospheric depth. Their literal hues belong to separate mood profiles.
The original sunny meadow remains a guide to varied, plentiful vegetation and
colour accents. Do not average all these moods into one universal palette.

What is visible in the references
1. Colour has roles. Moss and olive greens form the base. Yellow-green and
   cream highlights occupy selected surfaces. Dark olive, forest green and
   blue-green form the shadows. Bark brings restrained rose-brown warmth;
   litter and some plants repeat coral/ochre accents. Cool blue-grey/cyan
   distance separates layers. Small flowers are accents, not equal competitors.
2. Warm/cool contrast is relative. Not every shadow is teal, and not every
   leaf tip is gold. In the green woodland, some shaded foliage is dark olive.
   The path and sunset references use stronger blue-green shaded regions.
3. Brightness is distributed in connected patches: branch faces, fern fans,
   grass edges, strips of bark and pools on the path. Large dark masses remain
   alongside the light. It is the distribution, not just the sun's energy,
   that creates the sense of sunlight.
4. Form survives the colour. Broad lit faces, intermediate foliage and darker
   interiors describe clumps and overlapping branches. At reduced size these
   remain readable shapes, rather than a field of equally bright leaves.
5. The ground participates. Moss, ferns, litter, rocks and roots receive the
   same light colours as the trees. Ground accents repeat across several
   clumps. It does not read as one dark green carpet beneath yellow trees.
6. Depth is selective. Foreground retains contact and texture contrast;
   successive forest layers lose contrast and take on cooler atmospheric
   colour. Bright haze can be strong behind a subject without veiling it.
7. Painterly character is visible in coherent shapes, simplified planes and
   pigment variation. The references also contain fine detail and grain.
   They do not justify globally blurring textures or smoothing every edge.

Current-image assessment
Evidence: FloraTrail/Sunlight/game_saved_road.png, the saved sunlight correction.
The crowns remain broadly similar yellow-olive masses. The new direct sun
improves visible sunlight, but it also raises whole crown regions toward
yellow without consistently producing the reference's colour/form separation.
The undergrowth is predominantly dark green, with much weaker readable warm
accents. Bark is mainly brown; the prominent rocks read separately as mottled
grey. These are relationships to improve, not proof of wrong source textures.
Some sun patches and dark crown interiors already work; preserve them.
The open road composition contains substantially more sky than the forest
references. That must not be mistaken for a shading error. A whole-image
histogram or average RGB comparison would be misleading. Interior depth must
be judged from an under-canopy view, not certified from this open road image.

Acceptance criteria: visual gates, not an automatic similarity percentage
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
A. PALETTE HIERARCHY
Pass: green/olive remains the dominant vegetation identity; sunlight introduces
warmer local faces. Restrained coral/ochre accents visibly recur in ground
plants/litter. Cool shades and distance support the vegetation, rather than
requiring an unrelated global tint.
Fail: nearly every crown has the same mustard fill, all ground plants merge
into one green, or arbitrary per-leaf hue noise replaces intentional patches.
Current: fail / insufficient palette cohesion.

B. CROWN FORM AND COLOURED HIGHLIGHTS
Test: select three representative crowns, including both tree families, in
the open-edge and side-lit views; examine colour and greyscale thumbnails.
Pass: each crown has recognisable connected light-facing and shaded masses;
intermediate values preserve overlap and volume. Lit foliage retains pigment
and detail. This must be visible at a 320px-wide view as well as full size.
Fail: a crown reads as one flat bright cutout, or brightness erases its colour.
Current: partial; sunlight is present, but large uniform fills remain.

C. SHADE WITH READABLE COLOUR
Test: inspect fixed shaded fern, shrub and trunk crops beside nearby lit ones.
Pass: plant families and overlapping forms remain identifiable in shade, with
colour still visible. Cooler green/blue-green relationships should be evident
where the selected reference supports them; darkest contacts remain limited.
Fail: large featureless black regions, grey wash, glowing unshadowed foliage,
or making all shadow pixels uniformly blue.
Current: partial; dark regions exist, but their colour separation is limited.

D. COHERENT LIGHT ACROSS MATERIALS
Test: one frame must contain leaf, fern, bark, rock and ground under the same sun.
Pass: a sun patch reads consistently as warm on all five; their shaded sides
belong to the same environment while retaining their individual base colours.
Light pools and branch shadows remain recognisable on the road and understory.
Fail: attractive trees beside disconnected grey rocks, orange-only bark or
ground cover that appears unaffected by the surrounding light.
Current: fail / mixed material appearance still lacks cohesion.

E. DEPTH WITHOUT A FOREGROUND VEIL
Test: under-canopy camera with visible foreground, middle forest and background.
Pass: all three layers separate; the near plants retain clear form and contact,
middle trunks remain distinct, and far layers shift cooler with reduced contrast.
Fail: increasing fog flattens all layers, or distant trees retain near contrast.
Current: not established by the selected open-road image. Requires this view.

F. PAINTERLY READ AT TWO SCALES
Pass: at 320px width, foliage reads as coherent coloured masses; at native
resolution, source texture character and leaf/fern shape remain visible. During
a slow camera move, temporal noise must not become the dominant texture.
Fail: detail only looks appealing when blurred, sharpened grain dominates, or
the palette looks correct only in a carefully chosen still.
Current: partial for still images; movement is not evaluated by this analysis.

G. CONSISTENCY ACROSS THE REVIEW SET
Pass: A-D and F hold in an open road/forest edge, an under-canopy path and a
mixed-material bank view. E holds in a view with the required depth layers.
The same profile works with front/side/back lighting without per-camera
exposure or colour-grade fixes. A separate valley camera checks that distance
does not collapse the palette or canopy; these close references cannot prove
an exact aerial-view match.
Fail: tuning one hero screenshot breaks another required view.
Current: not tested under this rubric. No pass is inferred from earlier tests.

How we will review a future candidate
1. Preserve the current scene and the approved source-material bench as two
   distinct baselines. Capture one candidate at a time, changing one group of
   controls at a time. Preserve camera, FOV, resolution, exposure, display
   transform and render quality for each before/after pair.
2. Record actual native capture resolution; do not upsample evidence and claim
   a higher rendering resolution. Wait for shader and texture loading and
   temporal accumulation. Use a shared wind state for still comparisons.
3. Capture the three local views above plus a valley regression view. Compare
   semantic crops (lit/shaded foliage, bark, rocks, ground), not unrelated
   full-frame histograms. Supply greyscale thumbnails alongside colour.
4. Record each gate as pass, partial, fail or not tested, with a screenshot
   and one-sentence reason. Partial/not tested does not count as passed.
   No averaged score may hide a failed palette, shadow or material-cohesion gate.
5. Use a slow camera pass to review shifting hues, leaf stability and whether
   the painterly appearance persists. Do not quietly retune exposure per view.
6. Present actual Unreal/reference views for visual acceptance before widening
   the LiDAR rollout. A technical source-material match is not aesthetic approval.
7. Keep the 60 fps target as a subsequent measured performance gate at specified
   resolution/hardware. This analysis provides no new timing claim and authorizes
   no added rendering cost as a substitute for fixing the palette.

Next implementation order, after this target is established
First isolate why lit crowns become uniformly yellow: compare neutral lighting,
current daylight, and direction changes with pigment/normal/display response
examined separately. Preserve the source-parity benchmark as a diagnostic.
Then establish material-family colour roles across leaves, ferns, shrubs, bark,
rock and soil; use restrained variation across clumps, not random colour speckle.
Next tune selective direct light and coloured fill while holding exposure fixed.
Only then tune depth haze and a small final colour grade. Re-test the entire
review set after every candidate; do not use global desaturation or stronger
sunlight to hide a material-family mismatch.

Evidence and limits
Art/analyse_flora_palette.py creates local analysis figures and records the
source paths and sample boxes in Art/Export/FloraPaletteStudy/reference-evidence.json.
Sampled swatches are medians of marked patches in finished compressed images.
They include local light, texture, grading and compression. They are display
RGB, not linear material inputs, and are not mandatory hex-code targets.
The images alone cannot uniquely reveal sun energy, renderer settings, material
nodes or the proportion of painted versus simulated shading. All appearance
claims above are visual observations; implementation causes remain hypotheses
until controlled tests distinguish them.
