DESIGN DECISION

Build a lush, stylized Southland river gully with exposed weathered rock structure,
asymmetric bank-grown ferns, warm gravel, amber shallows and readable forest light.
The owner's site photos determine place, species character, river proportions,
surface relationships and ecological arrangement. The supplied colourful woodland
reference determines clarity, grouped colour, shape simplification and visual
richness. The selected rocky-bank direction remains: rock emerges through the
bank, with dense vegetation occupying soil pockets, cracks and sheltered shelves.

The existing LiDAR landform, recognizable site layout, road routes, crossings,
native mud/soil behaviour, water occupancy and interactions remain the foundation.
Local authored detail below DEM resolution must have consistent physical support.

EVIDENCE AND DIAGNOSIS

Confirmed in source:
- native_plant_lib.py::frond_path clamps p.z to floor when floor is supplied.
- build_native_bush.py crown ferns and build_southland_floor.py kiokio pass
  floor=.012. Kiokio's earlier shape fitting seeks hanging outer tips, but final
  frond construction supplies that horizontal floor. This is a concrete mismatch
  between a desired hanging form and a flat-ground construction assumption.
- lidar_bank_planting.py fits whole plant transforms and scales fern height by
  .82 while changing lateral proportions. This cannot independently control
  crown attachment, upright growth, uphill clearance and downhill hanging fronds.
- Foliage material code already enables Two Sided Foliage. RS1 raises its radial
  normal blend to .32. Enabling two-sided foliage again is not a diagnosis.
- The saved v6 preset uses 1600x900 output, TSR75 and StaticMeshLODDistanceScale 4;
  component-level LOD scales also exist. Actual runtime mesh/material assignments,
  LOD transitions and temporal rendering must be inspected, not inferred from names.
- RS6 used the bank's world-space pigment but still looked detached: shared colour
  code did not solve mismatched geometry, texture contrast, transitions or lighting.

Observed in captures:
- Broad smooth dark bank, visibly separate repeated plants and severe loss of
  colour information in shade.
- Rejected pass replaced that with oversized, similarly prominent rock objects,
  bare gaps and small isolated shrubs on tops. Much of the original vegetation
  structure was removed to accommodate the new objects.
- Technical placement checks passed without demonstrating visual coherence.

Not yet established by controlled runtime tests:
- Relative contribution of geometry, live LOD choice, normal modification,
  subsurface lighting, occlusion and TSR to the flat/speckled plant appearance.
- Main cause of excessive darkness: exposure, local exposure, actual canopy
  occlusion, skylight/GI representation or material values.
- Exact local bedrock geology. Do not invent conspicuous stratigraphy from a
  convenient asset pack. Follow the observed grey weathered rock character.

1. ESTABLISH A RELIABLE BASELINE

Use the restored map and saved v6 gameplay preset on the target RTX4050 Laptop
6GB. Record actual console values and material/LOD assignments at the chosen bank.
Capture standing eye height across the river, close oblique bank view, along-bank
view and ordinary vehicle approach. Include a short moving sequence, not only
high-resolution stills. Allow exposure, streaming and temporal histories to settle.

Perform controlled comparisons on the same plant and camera: current vs LOD0;
neutral material vs final material; geometric normals vs current modified normals;
wind off vs on; native-resolution diagnostic vs saved TSR75. Inspect base colour,
world normals, lighting-only, Lumen Scene and Surface Cache. Change one variable
per comparison and restore the shipping preset afterwards.

Deliverable: evidence of which faults belong to modeling, placement, materials,
lighting or temporal rendering. Geometry failure leads to a mesh fix. A good LOD0
that fails farther away leads to LOD work. A good neutral render that fails lit
leads to shading/lighting work. Do not treat every fault as a scatter problem.

2. FIX THE COMPOSITION AND LIGHTING TARGET BEFORE DETAIL

Choose one approximately 20 m bank section, including its river toe, full bank
height, canopy edge and a little foreground water/gravel. This is a bounded edit
in the real map, with temporary grouping and easy rollback, not a detached demo.
Exact bounds follow local cross-sections and protected crossing/road corridors.

Block it with simple forms: one dominant exposed rock/earth feature, secondary
interrupted exposures, a sheltered fern pocket and an open wet/gravel toe. Vary
height, depth and overlap. Preserve major trees; move or remove individual trees
only where there is a clear geometric or compositional reason.

Calibrate light in this simple scene: fixed exposure for comparisons; readable
cool/neutral shade; warm sun patches; bright foliage accents; retained dark
crevices. Audit sky capture, occlusion and Lumen coverage first. A small plausible
canopy opening may help, but mass tree clearing and blanket ambient increases are
not substitutes for lighting diagnosis. Verify the result under gameplay exposure.

Acceptance: the bank has a convincing silhouette and clear depth without texture
detail or decorative plants. Shadows retain distinctions between soil, leaves and
rock. Neither washed-out light nor crushed shade is acceptable.

3. AUTHOR A CONNECTED BANK STRUCTURE

Use the DEM for the broad landform, then build a small map-owned mesh set for
features it cannot describe: an embedded face/rib, a fractured shoulder/ledge and
an eroded soil/root transition. Fit these to the actual cross-section. Rock bodies
continue into the slope; visible exposures belong to one underlying formation.
Detached stones are subordinate pieces collected near the foot, scaled against
the river, vehicle and vegetation. No automatic 9-12 m tall versions of small rocks.

Major shape first, medium fractures second, surface grain last. Avoid complete
isolated silhouettes, evenly spaced outcrops, repeated top shelves and identical
rock heights. Blend the structural mesh into soil with actual overlap, tapered
earth shoulders, root mats and localized moss. Texture blending cannot hide an
incorrect outline or unsupported overhang.

Use closed geometry and deliberate collision. Any change to drivable ground must
use the existing relief/physics path. Preserve road/crossing clearance using full
footprints, not just pivot distance. Keep native water/soil queries consistent.

4. BUILD THE MISSING PLANT GROWTH FORMS

Create map-owned variants rather than destructively changing the shared plant kit:
- Upright crown fern for supported benches and forest floor.
- Asymmetric bank kiokio: short uphill fronds, rising central fronds, long outer
  fronds that arch away and hang below the crown; no flat-floor clamp below the edge.
- Small crevice fern: compact attachment, directional open silhouette.
- Wet-margin rush/sedge form for suitable sediment pockets, with bowed outer leaves.
- Irregular woody shrub, used sparingly where there is enough soil volume.
Moss/root mats are surface-attached growth, separate from these rooted plant forms.

Separate the attachment frame from the growth frame. Roots and the lower crown
fit the supporting pocket; main growth retains an upward component; individual
fronds respond to gravity, available space and bank aspect. Do not rotate the
entire crown to the terrain normal or flatten it to prevent intersection.

Check each form from above, eye level and below on flat, moderate and steep test
surfaces. Root probes validate contact; additional samples along representative
fronds check uphill clipping and believable overhang. Fold and twist leaves for
volume, preserve useful gaps, and differentiate young/old fronds. Build lower LODs
around the same crown height and hanging silhouette before reducing small leaflets.

5. PLACE PLANT COMMUNITIES WITH CAUSE

Annotate eligible growth points with support type, soil depth class, slope,
exposure, moisture/flood band, available volume and dominant outward direction.
Use these to select growth forms. Establish irregular patches first, then place
overlapping individuals within them. Species, age and colour vary within bounded
families; they are not independently randomized at every point.

Bank toe: wet stone, interrupted cobbles, sediment, sparse suitable margin growth.
Exposed faces: readable rock, roots, moss in damp recesses, localized crevice plants.
Shelves/hollows: dense interlocking ferns and accumulated organic matter.
Upper bank: deeper rooted shrubs, saplings, trunks and litter connected to the
forest behind. Trees grow from plausible soil volumes, not every available top.

Use the actual flow layout to place erosion, deposits and snagged timber. Debris
collects at obstructions and flood marks; moisture follows seeps/splash and drainage.
Leave intentional quiet areas around dominant forms. Density comes from layered,
overlapping communities, including low connecting vegetation, not uniform coverage.

6. BRING MATERIALS INTO ONE VISUAL LANGUAGE

Make a small in-scene material board under the same sun and shade. Establish
relationships between weathered grey-brown rock, warm pale gravel, umber wet soil,
olive/deep green mature foliage, lighter fresh growth and restrained bronze accents.
These are relational art targets; shader RGB values are chosen in-engine.

Control texture frequency, normal strength, roughness and saturation together.
Use broad readable planes and colour masses, restrained medium detail and fine
grain that appears mainly close up. Dampness modifies roughness and value in
spatially credible bands. Moss is broken and moisture-led, never a uniform green cap.

Match contact zones across terrain, rock, root pockets and foliage bases. Start
with existing world-space fields and authored vertex masks. Evaluate localized
RVT blending only if an actual seam remains; do not introduce a new large rendering
dependency before establishing the geometry/material target.

Retain leaf-specific normals and restrained light transmission. Verify per-instance
pivots in normal shaping. Check small plant contact shadows against their cost;
root darkness comes from structure and material transitions, not black circular
decals. Keep stems/crowns anchored during wind animation.

7. PROVE THE SECTION, THEN EXPAND

The first deliverable is a complete in-map section: structure, ground transitions,
three-dimensional foliage, coherent materials, lighting and moving-camera evidence.
Show matched baseline/final images and close, oblique, across-river and vehicle views.
No entire-bank deployment on the strength of a numerical placement report.

Expand through distinct arrangements: exposed rocky shoulder; damp fern recess;
root-bound forest edge; depositional gravel margin. Each has different massing,
canopy density, moisture and plant proportions. Use authored controls with bounded
procedural variation. Do not tile a complete rock-and-plant diorama along the river.

Carry the same visual rules through pasture -> rank grass -> scrub -> forest edge,
gravel -> compacted wheel lanes -> muddy drainage pockets, and dry bank -> damp toe
-> shallow amber water. Existing road and water gameplay stay functional.

8. MAKE THE AUTHORING REPEATABLE

Consolidate this bank treatment into one recipe/manifest with separate geometry,
growth, material and lighting parameters. It owns only the bounded region and
map-owned assets. Rebuild from stable source placements and explicit exclusions,
not repeated scale/tilt/removal passes over the last result. Record backups and
restore exact transforms on removal. Coordinate forest visuals and colliders.
Retain all unrelated work and the one-Unreal-process guard.

9. ACCEPTANCE AND PERFORMANCE

Visual acceptance:
- Bank reads as a continuous landform with exposed structure from every test view.
- No row of equally important boulders, potted shrubs or circular fern discs.
- Roots disappear into believable soil/crevices; upright and hanging foliage forms
  are legible, including across the river at normal gameplay resolution.
- Material scale and contrast agree across rocks, ground, wood and plants.
- Shade retains colour and depth; highlights preserve foliage shape.
- Plant groups create density and variation without obvious repetition.
- Movement does not reveal distracting LOD collapse, shimmer, ghosting or floating.

Technical acceptance:
- Correct saved/reloaded transforms, support, collision and crossing clearances.
- Terrain/water/road invariants preserved except explicitly authored local relief.
- Rebuild and rollback repeatable with no cumulative edits.
- Target hardware, resolution and saved v6 preset retained for comparison.
- Measure baseline and final GPU/frame timings, p95, VRAM and hitching during the
  same walk/drive. A provisional additional GPU budget of roughly 1 ms for the
  representative view is a design constraint to validate, not a promised result.
- If over budget, simplify small leaf detail, shadowing and distant clusters first;
  preserve important silhouettes. Do not claim 120 fps or lower global quality to
  disguise cost. Profile actual gameplay, not a memory-pressured duplicate editor world.

NZ Plant Conservation Network: kiokio occurs on cliffs, ravines and riverbanks;
its fronds may be erect or pendulous. Supports a bank-specific growth form.
https://www.nzpcn.org.nz/flora/species/parablechnum-novae-zelandiae/

Environment Southland: plant choice depends on site conditions, including wet/dry
positions and exposure. Supports habitat-based placement rather than random mix.
https://www.es.govt.nz/environment/land-and-soil/land-management/riparian-management

Epic stylized demo: coherent treatment across grass, stone, leaves and trunks.
Historical art-direction example, not a recommendation to copy its old renderer.
https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/new-release-stylized-demo

Epic UE5.8 foliage shading: transmission and subsurface colour; audit existing
implementation rather than enabling a feature the project already uses.
https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/shading-models-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US

Epic Lumen: inspect Lumen Scene/Surface Cache and tracing representation when
diagnosing lighting mismatch; do not infer the cause of dark banks from a screenshot.
https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine

Epic TSR: thin geometry can require special temporal handling. Validate actual
feature eligibility and motion behaviour; do not treat it as a geometry repair.
https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/thin-geometry-detection-with-temporal-super-resolution

Epic RVT: landscape/object blending is available as an optional seam solution;
evaluate only after the existing system and geometry are proven insufficient.
https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/runtime-virtual-texturing-in-unreal-engine
