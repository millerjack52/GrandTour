# 2026-10-06

## Living mud LM3b: the God of War look on snow, the snow feel, the snow throw

The snow strip (L_SnowDrive) has its look and its feel. The patch's material is procedural: fresh snow at 0.85 with a
little blue where it turns from the sun, a soft grain at 2, 12 and 60 cm, a glint in 1 cm cells that catch the sun,
packed tyre floors greyer-blue and smoother with the tyre's tread at 30 degrees, walls a shade darker blue-grey, and a
soft subsurface (the project runs Substrate, so it is a Substrate Slab with Wrap subsurface). The strip's landscape takes
the same fresh-snow response at the patch's edge and fades to grass over 3 m.

- No game and no other Unreal ran at any step, so the cost numbers count.
- **The feel.** In snow the tyre's own sinkage now rolls at the Dirt row's rate (the Snow row's counted the snow twice;
  it still applies outside snow patches). Tuned at full throttle by trials on the strip: SnowPlough 0.78 and the belly's
  snow drag off (0). With any belly drag the car fell into a 1.7-1.9 km/h crawl through the drift and stayed in it.
  Pass 1 in fresh snow: 9.3 km/h on the 35 cm, 5.6 km/h through the drift, never stopped; pass 2 in its trench 5.1x
  faster through the drift. The owner names the numbers.
- **The throw** from a wheel in snow goes through the spray's white snow puffs now (2,362 blobs on pass 1, all snow),
  and its clods are snow clods.
- **Samplers.** The glint: 846 pixels over 245 in a box of sunlit fresh snow, 0 with the glint off. The border: no dark
  or sky-coloured pixels; 2 levels between the snow just inside the edge and the landscape just outside it. That needed a
  fix: Substrate measures the subsurface's distance against the Slab's 0.01 cm thickness, not 100 cm, so the first runs
  had a near-white subsurface and a 10-16 level step at the edge.
- **Cost.** The base pass is 0.06 ms cheaper with the snow than with the clay on the same patch, but the whole GPU is
  +0.85 ms: deferred lighting +0.39 (with the subsurface on or off; cause unchecked), Lumen reflections +0.10, the
  subsurface's lights +0.25. The field's own cost passes (Settle p99 0.32 / 0.45 ms, clods 0.20 / 0.22).
- The six mud and snow tests pass. Suite: the targeted six 5 PASS and the full suite 27 PASS; terrain fails both times on its known flaky row alone, step 11's held exit in the bog (the snow changes do not reach it). The Hide's and the Proving Ground's biome hashes are unchanged.
- **Unchecked.** Every look (the owner's gate: drive it in Clear and in Snow weather). The tread stripes show across the
  belly's channel as well as the tyres' floors. The mud strip's black patches (not in LM3b's scope).
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM3b note), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 70, docs/LIVE_SNOW_DRIVE.md.

## Living mud LM3c: snow polish before the owner drives it

Three things the owner had already turned down on the mud were showing on the snow: tread stripes across the whole
packed floor, a smooth rounded rail for the belly's berm, and snow clods that read as dark stones. LM3c goes at all three.

- No game and no other Unreal ran at any step, so the cost numbers count. Nothing in the feel was retuned.
- **Tread.** The lugs are drawn only where a tyre has been: the belly's channel no longer carries them (the test finds no
  tread in the channel cells no tyre reaches). Where the belly scraped there are faint streaks along the direction of
  travel instead. The lugs are broken: each cell at a strength between a quarter and full, a fifth of the cells with none,
  in patches. The first check's streaks broke at every 12.5 cm cell into a faint grid; fixed by giving them a direction
  binned to 5 degrees.
- **The berm** is now built like a tyre's bank: waves along it, clumps, and lump domes, with nothing smoothing it. Its
  crest varies along the trench far more than LM3b's (coefficient of variation 0.78 against 0.16 in the test, 0.54 on the
  strip; asked at least 0.35). But that comes only from the lumps (0.22 without them), and they stack single cells into
  tall sharp peaks: up to 93 cm over the snow top in the test, 56-59 cm on the strip. The close picture shows the far
  berm as a row of sharp triangular peaks. Not tuned; it goes to the main session before the owner looks.
- **Clods** are white now (0.82) and share the snow's soft subsurface, so their shaded side is no longer a dark stone. In
  the sun they read at 0.92 of the snow round them (asked within 15 %).
- **The extra lighting cost** (+0.36 ms) is not Substrate's tile classification: the snow's pixels are classed as the
  heavier "Single" tiles only while its subsurface is on, and the cost is there with it off too. The cause is still open.
- The feel holds: pass 1 at 9.8 km/h in 35 cm and 5.5 km/h through the drift without stopping, pass 2 5.2x faster
  through the drift.
- Tests: the six mud and snow tests pass (the mud field's fingerprint is unchanged). Suite (terrain, drivetrain, grip, mud, surface, track): 5 PASS; terrain fails on its known flaky row alone, step 11's held exit in the bog.
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM3c note), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 71, docs/LIVE_SNOW_DRIVE.md.

## Living mud LM3c follow-up: the berm's peaks

The main session decided how to take the peaks off the snow berm. A lump that grows past its cap now gives its excess
back to the whole bank it came from, spread the way the bank itself is shaped, instead of stacking it onto one cell. That
is true for mud too. The berm also gets its own lump share (0.25, half the lips').

- No game and no other Unreal ran at any step.
- **No more towers.** In the test, the berm's tallest point fell from 93 cm to 30 cm over the snow. On the strip it fell
  from 56-59 cm to 31-32 cm, against a typical 17-18 cm.
- **One target can't be met.** The berm was asked to keep its 90th-percentile height within 1.3x its typical height. No
  setting of the berm does that. The tyres' own lumps lie under the berm, and on their own they already read 1.32x. With
  the berm's swings at 0.4 of the lips' (a number of mine, not the plan's), the tallest point keeps within 1.6x (1.53x),
  but the 90th percentile reads 1.40x. Its variation along the trench (0.175) is close to the old smooth berm's (0.16), well
  under the 0.35 asked. That test row is left failing for the main session to decide.
- **Mud.** The mud's fingerprint test (run with no lumps) is unchanged. Where mud lumps overflow, the banks shift a
  little: one test's bank step went from 18.68 to 18.83 cm. All five mud tests pass, and the live mud check runs clean.
- **The dark dashes** along the tyre slot in the close-up picture repeat every 9 cm: they are the tyre's own tread, not the
  12.5 cm grid of the snow's cells.
- The feel holds: 9.6 km/h in 35 cm, 5.5 km/h through the drift, no stop, 5.2x faster on the second pass.
- Suite: 5 PASS; terrain fails on its known flaky row alone, step 11's held exit in the bog.
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM3c note's follow-up), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 71.

## Living mud LM4: God of War snow deformation

The owner said the snow drives well but the cut doesn't look like God of War's: the edge should break into blocks, the
surface should be granular, the trail uneven, the shade grey rather than blue. LM4 builds the six pieces the plan named.
God of War Ragnarok was running the whole time, so the drive check, the new pictures and the cost measurement could not run: they are
owed before the owner drives. The snow's feel numbers are unchanged.

- **A finer grid for snow.** The snow's cells are 6.25 cm now (mud stays at 12.5), and the snow mesh follows them. Every
  number that was counted in cells was checked so it keeps the size it was meant to have; the table is in the plan's LM4
  note. A switch, `gt.Snow.FineGrid 0`, builds snow at the old 12.5 cm for comparison (restart Play). The mud is untouched:
  its fingerprint test reads the same.
- **Crust plates.** The lumps on the cut's lip are now flat slabs: each of the scattered lump spots owns its own patch of
  ground, and its snow is a plate tilted 5-25 degrees, with a sharp step to the next. Half the spots are left empty, in
  patches. The test fitted 74 plates: all between 5 and 25 degrees, leaning every which way.
- **A broken wall line.** The edge of the cut steps in and out by 3-8 cm every 15-30 cm. About one segment in seven drops
  its plate into the trench, leaving a notch, with a block on the floor now and then.
- **Snow shards.** Eight angular slab pieces, 8-22 cm across, made in Blender, replace the clods on snow: along the plates'
  edges and on the trench floor where a plate fell. The test counts as many as LM3c had clods, a third on the floor.
- **A granular material.** Small clumps (1.5 and 6 cm) with darker gaps on the snow, a faceted look on the walls and
  plates, and the blue shade halved toward grey (one dial, `SnowShadeSaturation`, for the owner to name).
- **An uneven snow top.** The fresh snow's surface rises and falls a few cm over 0.6-3 m, so the trench's depth varies along
  it; the drive reads the same uneven top.
- **One change the plan didn't name**: the old smoothing of the lip is off on snow. On the stepped lip it was losing
  snow (the compaction test read 0.8115 instead of 0.8), and the owner doesn't want smoothing.
- Tests: all seven mud and snow tests pass, the new God of War rows included.
- Owed: the drive check (the feel, `Snow_GoW_View.png`, `Snow_OnFoot.png`, the cost of the finer grid) and the targeted
  suite, once no game is running.
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM4 note), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 72, docs/LIVE_SNOW_DRIVE.md.

## Living mud LM4b: soft snow shape, crumbly detail in the surface

The owner drove LM4's snow and found it "a bit blocky", and sent a frame breakdown of God of War, where the trench's shape is
a smooth field and the crumbly look comes from detail drawn per pixel. LM4b makes that split. God of War Ragnarok started at
14:25 and ran to the end, so the drive check (the feel, every picture, the cost) and the suite could not run: they are owed,
with LM4's owed items. The snow's feel numbers and the mud are untouched.

- **The wall line** no longer steps in square notches: it wanders smoothly in and out by about 3 cm (two waves, 25 and 70 cm
  long). The test: no step over 1.3 cm between neighbouring 6.25 cm points along it (LM4: 15-17 cm).
- **Soft lumps.** LM4's flat tilted plates are rounded domes again, 5-12 cm in radius, leaning slightly, kept low (at most
  0.3 of their radius high) so their sides stay soft. Where a piece of the lip fell into the trench, the notch's ends fade
  out instead of cutting square. The test: steps over 3 cm in the lip band 3.3 % of cell pairs (LM4 7.8 %).
- **Rounded clumps** replace the shards on the snow: eight lumpy snow clumps, 5-18 cm, made in Blender, as many as LM4 had.
- **A snow-clump texture** made by a script (no download, nothing painted): small crumbs and larger clods as a height map,
  laid over the snow in world space. Fresh snow gets faint crumbs; the trench's edges, walls, lip and berm get crumbs and
  clods at full strength, picked out by an "edge mask"; the packed floor stays smoother with its tread. LM4's crisp cells and
  faceted walls are gone.
- **Parallax**, an experiment: the edges' detail can be drawn with depth (`gt.Snow.Parallax 1`). It is off by default until
  its pictures and its cost are seen.
- **One row fails:** the lip's crest should vary at least 30 % (its cv). The +Y lip reads 0.19; with
  no lumps at all it reads 0.15, so only tall, steep lumps lift it, which is what the owner called blocky. The softness was
  kept and the main session decides the row.
- **A fix found on the way:** where a lump fell into the trench, the plough's berm kept stacking snow in that one cell (a
  41 cm tower in the test); it spreads over the berm now.
- Tests: 6 of 7 mud and snow tests pass (the crest row above), the mud's fingerprint unchanged.
- Owed: the drive check (the feel gates, the God of War, on-foot and new lip close-up pictures with parallax on and off, the
  cost of the fine grid and of parallax) and the targeted suite, once no game is running.
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM4b note), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 73, docs/LIVE_SNOW_DRIVE.md.

## Living mud LM4c: snow piles, not walls

The owner looked at LM4b's trench at eye level: "the form of the snow is too uniform, and it sticks up way too much, those
tall edges need to fall down into piles". Each side of the trench was one long cliff: the cut wall standing at about 70
degrees with the plough's berm stacked on top, the same height all the way along. LM4c lets the loose snow slump, lets the
wall fall in places after the car has gone, and breaks the berm into piles. God of War Ragnarok was running the whole time,
so the drive check (the feel, every picture, the cost) and the suite are owed; the tests ran.

- **Loose snow slumps.** The berm and the lip (the snow pushed up above the snow's surface) now settle to 50 degrees, so they
  no longer stand on the wall's edge as a cliff; some runs into the trench. The cut wall below has its own angle every
  40-80 cm, between 44 and 84 degrees, so it stands in some stretches and eases in others.
- **Walls fall after the car.** A fifth of the wall, in pieces 30-80 cm long, drops its top third to half 0.5-3 s after the
  last tyre cut it, as a pile on the trench floor at the wall's foot with a clump or two on it. In the test that is about 3.5
  pieces per 10 m of wall, piles up to about 12 litres.
- **The berm in piles.** The plough pushes its snow out wider (85 cm) and falling away from the trench's edge, and along the
  trench it comes in separate piles with low gaps between them, 1-2.5 m apart.
- **The numbers in the test**, against LM4b's: the edge (the berm's top) about 10.6 cm over the snow at the median and 16 cm
  at the 90th percentile (LM4b 19 and 29); about 5 piles per 10 m; the berm's height varies 36 % along the trench (LM4b
  27-29 %); 57 % of the outer wall still steeper than 55 degrees (LM4b all of it); 29 % of the trench floor's middle carries
  fallen snow 2 cm or deeper.
- **The plan's starting numbers moved**: the loose snow's angle 35 -> 50, the wall's 60 -> 64, the collapses 25 -> 20 %, the
  gaps between piles 0.2 -> 0.08, the berm's width 70 -> 85 cm. With the plan's own numbers the trench floor filled (56 %
  of it covered) and the piles smoothed out; the reasons and the trade-offs are in the plan's LM4c note.
- **Worth knowing:** several of the test's targets pull against each other (a wall that falls has to land on the floor of a
  trench only a tyre wide), so the landing sits close to their edges, and another stretch of snow reads a few points either
  side. The main session decides.
- Tests: 8 of 8 mud and snow tests pass (a new one, GrandTour.Snow.Piles), the mud's fingerprint unchanged.
- Owed: the drive check (the feel gates - the second pass now ploughs the snow that fell into the trench - the on-foot,
  God of War and edge pictures, a new before-and-after pair of a wall collapsing, the cost) and the targeted suite, once no
  game is running.
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM4c note), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 74, docs/LIVE_SNOW_DRIVE.md.

## Living mud LM4d: the belly's channel isn't flat

The owner drove LM4c: "the edges look great, but the middle is still very perfectly flat". The middle is the channel the car's
belly scrapes between the two tyre trenches - a level plane a metre wide. LM4d leaves LM4c's edges alone and roughens the
channel. God of War had closed by then, so the drive check ran for the first time since LM3c (it covers LM4, LM4b and LM4c too),
and so did the suite.

- **The scrape undulates**: the belly cuts to a height that rises and falls by up to 2.5 cm in long shallow waves along the
  trench, instead of one flat level.
- **Smear ridges**: low ridges of dragged snow, 1-3 cm high and up to 80 cm long, bunched, about three per metre, dropped out
  behind the belly from the snow it pushes aside (moved, not lost).
- **Spill**: 15 % of what the plough pushes to the sides falls back into the channel as soft lumps, with the odd snow clump.
- **The surface**: the crumbly snow detail at mid strength over the channel, and stronger scrape streaks.
- The test numbers: the channel's floor now varies with a typical spread of 2.4 cm per 1 x 3 m patch (LM4c: none); 2.9 ridges
  a metre; 11 % of the channel raised 2 cm or more by spill.
- **What the drive check found** (the first in five stages): the second pass through the drift is 4.3x the first (needs 1.15x),
  but the first pass is slow outside the drift: 5.6 km/h where 8-15 is asked, mostly in the stretch after the drift (3.7). At
  the coarser 12.5 cm grid it reads 8.8. The settling also costs far over its budget at 6.25 cm (1.5 ms a frame on average).
  Nothing in the feel was retuned; the main session decides.
- **In the pictures**: from eye level the channel still reads close to level with long streaks and a few lumps - the owner
  judges whether it is enough; the berm reads as separate soft piles; the wall-collapse pair shows a new clump and a changed
  foot of the wall after 4 s, but its camera doesn't show the notch.
- **One test row fails**: a God of War stage row counting snow chunks reads 42 % on the trench floor (20-40 asked); LM4d's
  ridges and spill shift the random draw that decides the chunks. Reported for the main session.
- Tests: 8 of 9 mud and snow tests pass (a new one, GrandTour.Snow.Channel), the mud's fingerprint unchanged; the suite 6 of 6.
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM4d note), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 75, docs/LIVE_SNOW_DRIVE.md.

## Living mud LM4e: the snow's cost

The owner asked for the snow to be made a lot cheaper without changing how it looks or drives. Two profiles ran on the snow
strip (each feature switched on and off in A-B-B-A order) before the owner stopped timing runs to test it himself; Blender
ran through parts of both, so only the on/off differences count.

- **Where the cost was.** Before: on the first pass Settle took 1.9 ms a frame on average (8.6 ms at worst), the texture
  upload 1.7 ms (65,000 cells a frame, most of them re-sent only for a fading sheen the snow material never draws), the
  stamps 1.2 ms. On the GPU the snow patch cost 7.8 ms from on foot (its shadows 3.4 ms of it) and 10 ms from the overview,
  against the clay bog's 3.7 ms.
- **The cuts**, each with a switch (on by default): the snow's tiles sleep as soon as they settle (`gt.Snow.QuietSleep`),
  Settle skips the flat pairs first, four at a time (`gt.Snow.FastSweep`), the upload sends only the 2 m tiles that changed
  (`gt.Snow.UploadTiles`), the snow mesh is built in 8 m chunks that go coarser with distance (`gt.Snow.MeshLod`), the snow
  material is opaque (its mask was always 1), and it skips the scrape-streak noise where there are none
  (`gt.Snow.FastMaterial`).
- **Measured before the stop**: on the same recorded drive Settle fell from 1.38 to 0.84 ms and the upload from 1.52 to
  0.53 ms; the chunked mesh saved 1.3 ms of GPU on foot. Pass 1 after the first five cuts: Settle 0.38 ms (asked 0.3), the
  upload 0.37 (asked 0.2), the frame 19.6 ms against the mud strip's 18.8 (allowed +2); the snow patch still costs about twice
  the clay patch on the GPU (asked at most +1 ms). The four-at-a-time sweep, the tile skip, the faster encoder and the material
  fast path came after and are unmeasured.
- **Nothing visible moved**: pictures of the same recorded drive differ by under half a level in 255 below the sky (the sky's
  clouds drift between sessions); the chunk borders add no dark or sky pixels; the snow tests read the same numbers (one
  internal fingerprint changed, its two runs still equal); pass 1 drove 9.3 km/h outside the drift, pass 2 3.9x faster
  through it.
- The owner's test list is in docs/LIVE_SNOW_DRIVE.md ("How to test the optimisation").
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM4e note), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 76.

## Living mud LM4f: the bigger snow cuts, behind switches

The owner wants the snow a lot cheaper and will do the timing himself, so this stage put in the cuts LM4e left out (each can
change the look or how the snow settles), every one behind its own switch and on by default, and ran no timing at all - only the
build, the shape tests and the strip's setup.

- **Shadows from a coarse copy** (`gt.Snow.ShadowProxy`): the snow surface no longer casts its own shadow; an invisible 25 cm
  copy of it does (about a sixteenth of the triangles). The shadows along the trench walls and the lip can shift by up to
  about 12 cm.
- **A cheaper snow material** (`gt.Snow.FastMaterial 2`): the crumb detail only on edges, walls and lips (fresh, untouched snow
  and the packed floors lose their faint crumbs), one projection on flat ground, one tiling on soft edges, no sparkle past 20 m
  and no soft blue subsurface past 35 m. `1` is LM4e's material, `0` the full one.
- **A faster settle** (`gt.Snow.FastSettle`): the snow's slumping is worked out 15 times a second instead of 30 (twice as far each time), only the
  snow within 2 m of something that changed in the last 3 s keeps settling, and the work is spread over the CPU's cores (with the
  same numbers every run). It fails one shape gate: a few more of the cut walls stay steep (62 % of the outer wall over 55
  degrees, asked at most 60), because walls stop settling 3 s after the car has gone. Left for the main session to decide.
- **Cheaper wheel stamps**, no switch: the fixed random patterns the stamps read are worked out once when the patch starts
  instead of on every stamp. Every test number came out identical to the digit, which proves nothing changed.
- GoW's chunk-share test now allows 20-45 % (it read 42 % before any of this).
- The owner's test list, with what each switch risks and an estimate of what it saves, is in docs/LIVE_SNOW_DRIVE.md ("How to test
  the optimisation").
- Details: docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM4f note, every moved test row), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 77.

## Living mud LM5b phase B: four churned bogs, one layer of mud

The owner wanted the ruts to look like the rest of the mud, only a little wetter, and four kinds of churned bog to pick per
stretch of road. Now they're in the game:

- **One layer.** A mud patch wears one churned-bog texture everywhere: untouched mud, ruts, ridges, banks and the clods. A rut
  only differs by its shape, a slight fresh sheen that dries off (half as strong as before), rain and the water standing in it.
  The old smoother "worked clay" in the tyre tracks, the matte crests and the darker middle are gone.
- **Four variants**, chosen per Mud section (`Mud Variant` in a terrain spec): **Standard** (the approved bog, the feel exactly
  as before), **Firm** (lighter, matte, small lumps; sinks less, grips more), **Deep** (darker, glossier, wetter; sinks half as
  deep again, the water stands 10 cm higher) and **Stony** (gravel pressed in; sinks a little less, grips most). The feel
  numbers are proposals for the owner to name after driving.
- **No visible repeat**: the texture is laid twice at sizes that never line up (3 m, and 4.3 m turned) and a soft noise picks
  between them; far-off glossy mud is made a little duller so the sky doesn't streak across distant ruts (switchable).
- **No seam at the edge**: the ground just around each patch takes the same variant's texture.
- **A new test strip, L_MudVariants**: Firm, Standard, Deep and Stony in a row on one gravel road. Driven once flat out in low
  range with the diff lock it finished; Firm went by at 32 km/h with the wheels 24 cm in, Standard at 24 km/h (40 cm), Stony at
  23 km/h (32 cm), and Deep was a crawl at under 2 km/h with the wheels 60 cm down and the belly dragging. Up close the lumps in
  Firm, Standard and Stony read like stones or pebbles set in mud; that's the owner's look to judge.
- Every existing level stays on Standard, so it drives the same; its mud now wears the Standard bog everywhere.
- No speed or frame-cost measurements were taken (the owner tests performance). The terrain test's bog escape failed once at
  6.9 m (8 m asked) and passed on its rerun (8.3 m): a gate at its edge, not this change.
- Details: docs/MUD_VARIANTS.md, docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md (the LM5b note), VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM section 78.
