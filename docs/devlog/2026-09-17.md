## Water stage WS2: the sources (VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM 42)

WS1 left the field simulated and still: a steady channel's shallow-water solution is geometrically steady, so the
motion probe read 0.17 % where W1's fake ripple read 13.16. WS2 gives the field the things that move it - the car,
the wind, the rain - and takes the two ends apart to see whether they can stop reflecting.

**The car as a lid.** `FGTWaterField` keeps a per-cell `LidM`, the metres of the water column a solid holds, and the
solver's surface becomes bed + depth + lid. Water is pushed out from under a lid until the effective surface
equalises, and runs back when the lid leaves; that is Archimedes with no bookkeeping, and the volume held to 0.274 %
through a crossing. `UGTHullComponent` stamps the body's box at `LidFill` 0.24 - HullVolumeM3 3.4 over the box's
14.2 m3, so the field pushes aside exactly the water W2's lift stands on - and each wheel in contact solid, biggest
stamp per cell wins, the bottom never below the bed, `LidRateMps` 1.5 m/s of change, `LidBlend` 0.25 spread into the
neighbours. Measured: a bow crest of 20.25 cm within 1.5 m ahead of the hull (asked over 5), a trough of -11.31 cm
within 2 m behind (asked under -3), 0.87 m3 of lid crossing and 0.69 parked.

**Three corrections, each measured, before the car felt what it used to.** A body reading its own escape jet took
21,274 N against W2's 3,009 and was pushed 0.83 m against its 0.5 m gate: a lidded cell now takes the stream at the
edge of the lid. Averaging that stream inward diffused it, because the flow divides around a car, and the carried car
drifted 2.25 m against W2's 4.52: it is copied now, and the hull's drag reads the free stream `FreeStreamM` 4 m
upstream, which is what a drag coefficient is defined against. And with no smoothing the four wheels' lids piled the
water they pushed into a ridge beside the car, which read 1.00 m of water over its keel in a 0.60 m ford and drowned
its own engine; `LidBlend` 0.25 is the least that keeps the car out of its own splash, and it costs the crests it
spreads (26.6 cm of bow crest unsmoothed, 20.3 at 0.25, 16.1 at 0.5).

**Wind and rain, both volume-neutral.** The wind drops a random velocity over a soft 1.5 m disc 60 times a second
over the whole field, gusting over 4 s, only on wet cells - a dipole, so it radiates as a ring and only fluxes are
touched. `WindRippleCm` had to go from the plan's 1.5 cm/s to 20: at 1.5 the roughness over the middle 10 x 10 m read
0.028 cm against the 0.5-2 the gate asks, and 24 patches landed in twelve seconds; at 20 it reads 0.750 cm over 269.
The rain is 20 drops per m2 of water per second at intensity 1, each 0.8 cm on one cell with the same volume off its
eight neighbours - 82,770 drops in twelve seconds, roughness 1.544 cm, volume held to 0.0158 %. The weather gained
`SetRainForTest`, which overrides only what `GetRainIntensity` and `GetRainAt` answer, so the water can be rained on
without soaking the world or putting drops in a picture.

**The ends: two mechanisms, both measured, both off.** The plan's sponge cannot work - the basin's own mode is sixty
metres long and a five-metre relaxation layer is a wall to it: 114 cm of swing pinned to the nominal head, 126 with
the target lagging, against ~100 with no layer at all. So the boundary was rebuilt as an open radiating (Flather)
end, its mean discharge learned while it is quiet, and that does work: the raise's swing fell to 18.5 cm. It costs
the steadiness the ford is tuned on - the loop chases the basin's 33 s seiche, the field breathes 1.3 % of its volume
every ten seconds against 0.2 pinned, the crossing needs 90 s to settle instead of 60 and reads 1.23 m/s instead of
1.29 - and the game never raises a ford from its ends, because a level that jumps is carried into the field whole.
So `bOpenEnds` is false and `SpongeTauS` 0, both one line to turn back on, and the three gates that read 0 are all
the same thing: a closed basin with mirrors for ends.

**A guard the stage needed.** `MaxFlowSpeedMps` 3.0 on any face. A lid puts a step of the whole depth across one
25 cm cell, 23 m/s2 of acceleration, and unguarded a cell empties its column into its neighbour in a sub-step and the
surface runs away. It bit about 1,900 times in a run; the depth guard beside it has never fired.

**The numbers.** Water test, 8 steps, 200 s: current 1.29 m/s at the crossing (WS1 1.30) with the volume at
0.21 %/0.35 %; bow 20.25 / -11.31 cm; the middle back within -1.75 cm of the level ten seconds later (asked 1, the
car's own wave still crossing the basin); standing crest 1.17 cm (asked 2); raise within 5 cm in 4.8 s then 103.6 cm
of swing (asked 5); ring as WS1 (1.87 s, 4.92 -> 1.71 cm, 0.0000 % of volume, the bank 1.81 cm) with the new
flow-wise end reading 0.56 cm of a 0.51 incident (asked 0.5); wind 0.750 against 0.000 off; rain 1.544; 0.910 ms mean
a stepping frame with everything on (WS1 0.570 with nothing), 3.248 worst. Terrain's six ford gates all read 1:
through in 0.75 m, flooded at 1.49, held 0.10 m, carried 6.43 m at cos 1.00 with the keel at 1.00 of a predicted
1.05, winched in 22.1 s. The full suite ran once, 21 PASS and 2 FAIL (water and terrain), and water and terrain were

**The pictures** are ten now. hide_23's bow box projects 18 corners where WS1's projected 0 - the car had never

## Water stage W3: one water model

The last stage with two water models in it. A ford's water came from UGTWaterSubsystem::Sample, a bog's rut water from
AGTMudPatch::WaterDepthCmAt behind a switch that was off, and two things answered "how deep is it here". Now one does:
EGTWaterKind gains Rut, every mud patch registers with the water subsystem at BuildPatches, and Sample answers the
deeper of the sheet and the patch - never the sum, flow zero in a rut, kind Rut anywhere inside the box. bRutWaterPhysics
is removed and so is the traction's max-of-two branch, so a wheel takes its depth from the one call and the rut water
counts in the drag, the buoyancy term and the flood. The hull ignores kind Rut on purpose: a rut is narrower than a
1.9 m hull and its water sits under the axle line, and a drag box reading it would be a new force on the bog's marginal
crawl.

Terrain step 10 now reads its rut water through the query - table -25.0, the deepest cell -30.8, asked 5.8 cm, read
5.8 cm, kind Rut - and a new sources line reads the whole model at once: Ford 61.4 cm mid-ford, Rut 5.8 cm in the
step's own rut, None 0.0 cm on the road at 100 m, and the hull's submerged height 0.000 m with the car standing in the
bog. 22 of terrain's 23 gates, the failure step 11's standing flake; step 11's other gate passed this run for a change.
Water is 7 of 9: the raise's 12.64 cm (the gate WS2 left open) and the bow's +1.10 cm against a gate of 1, a marginal

## W3 follow-up: is it the rut water?

The bog's numbers moved at W3 and "cause unmeasured" is not something to hand the user before they drive, so the rut
water got a switch - gt.Water.RutWater, default 1, and -GTRutWater=0 read once at the water subsystem's Initialize, so
a headless run can take the pre-W3 answer - and then four terrain runs, OFF, ON, OFF, ON, all 22 of 23 gates.

It is the rut water, and it is a bias rather than noise: both OFF runs sit together and both ON runs sit together. The
middle crawl's body sink 8 and 10 cm lower against 26 and 25; the mud field's rut mean -28.8 and -31.8 against -34.1
and -35.3; the escape's fastest 198 and 188 cm/s against 162 and 167, and its reverse 6.0 m in 6.5/5.1 s against 4.2
and 4.4 m in 8.0; step 11's first pass 5.2 and 5.2 s to 6 m against 6.4 and 6.3, and its held exit 5.0 and 4.4 against
5.6 and 5.8. Overlapping, so run-to-run: the slowest speed in the bog (100-104 against 102-103), the contacts past half

What it is not is the drag's arithmetic: 5.8 cm of water at WaterDragPerMetre 2.4 and 1 m/s is 0.0014 of the load. A

## W5: rivers rise with rain

A ford's level followed the ground's wetness, 80 cm at wetness 1, which is a puddle's clock. Now it follows the rain in
the catchment: `UGTWaterSubsystem` carries one river stage for every sheet in the world, advanced once a frame by the
first sheet that ticks, relaxing toward `RiverRiseCmAtFullRain` 80 x the weather's global rain intensity at `RiseLagS`
180 s rising and `FallLagS` 600 s falling. `RiseWithWetnessCm` is 0 and its term stays in the sum. The rise reaches the
surface as many tiny lifts of the whole field - every level change has been carried whole since WS2's follow-up - which
needed one line of that follow-up fixed: the sheet remembered the level every tick whether the change had been carried
or not, so a 0.007 cm rise was thrown away for ever, 0.007 cm at a time.

The new water step measures the rise path itself, with the lags scaled to a twentieth: nine seconds of rain leave the
stage at 50.57 cm of the 50.57 asked, the ford's middle rides within 3.36 cm of base + stage and swings 3.37 about that
moving line (both asked under 5), and thirty seconds of none leave 18.60 of 18.60. The middle is the mean of the middle
10 x 10 m, because the same rain that raises the river also stirs the surface 1.3-1.5 cm. The 70 cm `SetExtraRiseAll`
jump is a surge - a test and event device, not how water rises in play - so its swing keeps a sanity gate of 25 cm
(15.01 this run), and the water test PASSES all ten of its gates for the first time since WS2. Terrain fails only step
11's stage C held exit, the standing flake, with all six ford gates 1 and the ford as built still 0.75 m deep; weather
PASSES. `gt.Water.RiverLagScale` and `gt.Weather.Rain` are there for watching it happen. Not driven, not felt.


## W4: the look (the plan's last stage)

The user, on foot at the ford after WS2: "I'm really happy with it. The one thing to take note of that we will fix
later is getting the color/opacity of the water correct." So the ford's surface stops being a tint with procedural
shading on it. The field texture already carried the surface height and the water column; it now also carries the
cell's flow in its own axes, cm/s, in the two channels nothing was using. The colour runs `ShallowColor`
0.10 0.13 0.10 to `DeepColor` 0.02 0.035 0.03 over `WaterDeepCm` 120 and the opacity 0.25 to 0.85 with 0.30 more at a
grazing angle, all read per pixel out of the depth channel rather than from a `DepthFade` node - a shallow-water field
is the one place where knowing the depth is free. WS2's crest and slope shading stays on top as multipliers, because
it is what makes the motion at rest visible at all, and its gates still hold: the motion pair reads 34.34 % flowing
against 0.00 % still.

The ripple is a sourced CC0 normal ("Rain Ripples on Puddle", TextureCan.com, CC0 1.0) panned by the field's own
velocity as a flow map - two samples half a period apart, cross-faded on a 2 s sawtooth, two layers at 200 and 450 cm -
so it rides the current instead of stretching with it, and drifts at 8 cm/s where the water stands. ambientCG and Poly
Haven have no water texture at all, which is recorded in `Art/Textures/Water/SOURCE.txt` along with why 3dtextures.me
and OpenGameArt were not used.

The foam cost two of the plan's three numbers. At `FoamSlope` 0.15 and `FoamSpeedCmPerS` 120 the whole channel went
white - 41.19 % of the bank picture's water box over 200, against 1.88 % with the flow and wind off - because this
ford runs 143 cm/s at the crossing and WS2's wind puts 1.5 cm of roughness on 25 cm cells, which is a slope of
0.04 cm/cm. Both thresholds now sit above what a drivable crossing does on its own (0.6 and 250), the same box reads
15.32 %, and the user lowers them by name to get rapids.

The spray gained a fifth kind, water, additive to the mud it already throws on a ford's muddy bed: a wheel in more
than 0.10 m of the one query's water over 2 m/s throws 60 droplets a second scaled by speed x depth, and the hull
throws a bow spray off its front edge over 0.05 m submerged and 3 m/s. The spray's own test map has no water on it, so
the check is logged from the terrain ford steps: crossing as built, four wheels at once, the deepest in 0.79 m, 213
droplets a second, the bow spraying with 0.79 m submerged.

A new picture, `hide_29_ford_shallow`, stands on the near bank and looks down into the water where the bed shows
through - 21.3 cm of water a metre past the waterline, 62.5 cm five metres past it - for the colour judgement the user
asked for. Finding the waterline took a walk along the route: the Ford section starts at 1090 m and the water at
1110.5, and the first run of that picture framed fifty metres of dry bank. The water test's texture check now reads
the channels through the texel the upload writes, which is checkable under NullRHI, and reads the flow at step 1 where
the current runs rather than at step 8 where the test has calmed the water to 0.3 cm/s and a unit error would pass.

Two of the plan's foam numbers moved, both measured. Three builds, all exit 0; the setups were the textures and the
