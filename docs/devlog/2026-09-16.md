## Stage H: less spiky ruts, more randomness (and H0, the tread phase)

The user's two words after D and E: "make the ruts less spiky, and to increase randomness". Both are the field's
shape, so both were built and measured rather than looked at. `docs/VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM.md` 34 has all of it.

**H0 first** (a stage F follow-up). The lug phase ran with the wheel's own rolled distance, which under a low-range bog
crawl advances metres per centimetre of ground; it now runs with the contact patch's ground travel along the tyre's
heading (zero while the wheel is not turning). The tread picture no longer repeats at the field's 12.5 cm cell.

**H1** reads the field bicubic (Catmull-Rom, 16 taps) in the vertex shader and in the surface shader - where the slope
is the interpolant's own derivative over the same taps, not a second set of samples - and the stamp now blends a hump
into a crest cell by max plus `RidgeBlend` 0.9 of the lesser instead of summing, then pulls the cells it touched toward
a 3-tap mean along its own direction by `RidgeSmooth` 0.7, scaled by how far the stroke rolled.

**H2** varies the ridge's return by `RidgeNoise` 0.7 in clumps of 4 cells, the rut floor by `FloorNoiseCm` 1.5 at 5
cells and the tracks' edge at 3 cells, all from a hash of the cell index (no state); and both materials' shared
tri-planar block gets two octaves of world-space value noise at `MudNoiseFineCm` 60 and `MudNoiseCoarseCm` 250 which
warp the uv by `MudNoiseWarp` 0.15 tiles, swing the tint by `MudNoiseTint` 0.08 and the roughness by `MudNoiseRough`
0.1.

**The measurement had to be built twice and the code three times.** The test stamps a 3.6 m line in 18 uneven strokes
at a clean spot and reads the crest station by station (the highest cell across the hump), at three spots in one run:
as shipped, H1 alone, and stage H off. Off: mean 6.66 cm, cv 0.10, cell-to-cell difference sd 0.88. H1 alone: 6.16,
0.08, **0.47** (a fall of 47 %). Shipped: **6.96 cm, cv 0.21, rut floor -28.0 against -28.2**. What went wrong on the
way: a crest cell smoothed against whatever lay beside it drags the ground the wheel is about to drive into into the
rut (ridges -1.6 cm, 0 of 92, the fronts lowering a third of what they did); keeping "a stamp never raises" as a
`min()` turns the smoothing into a ratchet (a 75 cm rut at -41 with its crest inside it); and pulling each cell toward
a *noised* mean compounds by (1 + smooth x noise) every stamp, which reached infinity in ten seconds and killed Chaos
with a NaN. Two of the plan's defaults had to move: 0.5 and 0.35 at a 2-3 cell wavelength cancel each other (cv 0.07
against the 0.12 the old field had), because the smoothing and the noise are the same filter at that scale.

**Pictures.** `hide_13_rut_low` 266 dark of 28,302 on a 1.8 m crawl, and the 200-235 band (the specular spikes the plan
wanted gone) down to 4.4 % of the box from 6.1 % at stage E and 9.1 % at F. Border -10.9 (mud 185.4, landscape 196.3);
`hide_18_border_far` 0 dark 0 sky of 814. Tread: the axis lies on stamped rut for 38 of 89 samples (about 76 cm, 8.4
lugs), the field wraps 7 times and the picture reads 10 peaks - both within 2, against stage F's 12 against 7.3, and
the strongest repeat is no longer the cell. A look: the rims are rounded and wavy instead of saw-toothed and the crest
has broken into clumps with dark pockets.

**Suite: Terrain **FAIL**, on the stage C ruts gates of step 11 and nothing else: the pass down the ruts reached 6 m in **5.2 s against the first pass's 5.3** (the gate wants 5.05) and the 30-degree held exit in **4.8 s against 5.2** (it wants 5.46); the unheld exit took 4.1 s and ended 30 degrees off the ruts against the held one's 16, so the hold still shows in the heading. Reported as it comes, not tuned (movement).**

## Stage G: water in the ruts

`docs/MUD_ONE_SURFACE_PLAN.md` stage G, after stage I. Full write-up: `docs/VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM.md` section 36.

**What it is.** A Mud section carries a water table, `FGTRouteSection::WaterTableCm` **-20 cm** relative to the
undisturbed ground under each point, and the weather lifts it by `AGTMudPatch::WetTableLiftCm` **15 cm** x wetness as
`AGTWaterSheet` lifts a ford. The patch lays a second grid, `WaterMesh`, the same 158,730 vertices as the mud mesh but
each one the table over its rest height, translucent, sorted after the mud, no collision and no shadow. It wears a new
`M_GT_MudWater` (`Art/setup_m6_water.bat` + `Art/Unreal/setup_m6_water.py`, **17 expressions**), which reads the
patch's own field texture and whose opacity is `saturate((table - mud height) / EdgeFadeCm 1.0)`: a pixel whose mud
stands at or above the table is fully transparent, so the water is in the ruts and only in the ruts and its surface
follows the ground cell by cell. The tint runs 0.100 0.085 0.060 to 0.018 0.014 0.010 over `WaterDeepCm` 12, the
opacity 0.35 to 0.92 with `FresnelOpacity` 0.35 at a grazing angle, the roughness 0.06 to 0.02 there. The setup order
is now textures, **water**, mud, hide.

**The physics half is built and off.** `UGTMudSubsystem::bRutWaterPhysics` is false; with it on a wheel inside a patch
takes the deeper of the ford sheet's depth and the patch's `WaterDepthCmAt` into the water drag, the buoyancy and the
engine flood that were already there. Nothing in the driving reads it until the user asks.

**What it measured.** Terrain step 10's new `rut water` line, read through the call the traction will make: table
**-20.0 cm**, the deepest cell of the stamped crawl line **-30.8 cm** -> asked **10.8 cm**, the patch reports
**10.8 cm** (gate: within 2), that line's rut mean -29.6 -> 9.6 cm, untouched mud **0.00 cm**, the water grid
**158,730 vertices** against the mud mesh's 158,730, drawn -> **1**. The new picture `hide_21_rut_wet` (21 now,
hide_13's eye and box, the wetness forced to 1 for the shot and put back) read the table at **-5.3 cm** (-20.0 + a
lift of 14.7 at wetness 0.97).

**Two things worth saying plainly.** (1) Both picture runs stalled the crawl (**0.0 m and 0.1 m** against stage I's
4.0 m), so the band boxes are a seventh the size and the two runs disagree with each other by a factor of three -
`hide_13`'s 200-235 band read 3.7 % then 1.25 % against stage I's 3.2 %, `hide_20`'s 4.5 % then 3.9 % against 1.5 %.
Those moves are the crawl's, not the water's. (2) In the wet picture the water is a **dark fill**: 90-95 % of the box

belly's 10-20 cm smear and only a driven rut's floor gets past -20. Both are the user's to call.

**Terrain FAIL, on step 11's held exit alone** (6 m down the ruts 5.4 s against the first pass's 6.8; the 30-degree

## Water stage W1: a current in the ford

`docs/WATER_PLAN.md` stage W1, the first of the five that follow the parked mud. Full write-up:
`docs/VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM.md` section 39.

**What was built.** A Ford section carries `FlowSpeedMps` and `FlowYawOffsetDeg` (the flow's yaw is the section's
mid-route heading plus the offset, so 90 runs the water from the driver's left to his right); `AGTWaterSheet` carries
the direction and speed, fades them to zero over `FlowFadeM` 5 m at its two ends along the flow, and pushes `FlowDir`
and `FlowSpeed` onto its own material instance. A new `UGTWaterSubsystem` is the one water query - sheets register at
BeginPlay, `Sample(GroundPoint)` gives depth, surface height, current and kind - and the traction reads it in place of
`AGTWaterSheet::DepthCmAt`, which is now a wrapper over it. A new `UGTHullComponent` beside the traction treats the
body as a box on the wheels' contact plane and pushes it with `0.5 rho Cd A |v| v` at five sample points, capped at
1.5 g. The wheels' water drag now runs on the wheel's speed *through* the water, its along-car part in the old drag
term inside the old 0.9 cap and its across-car part as a force at the contact. `M_GT_Water` moved out of
setup_m6_hide.py into setup_m6_water.py (it was built once and only if missing, so changes never reached it) and gained

## Water W1 follow-up: the tell nobody could see

The user drove W1 and said "I didn't see any movement in the water". The wiring was right - the material has the ripple
on the normal, the sheet writes FlowDir and FlowSpeed every tick, the hide log shows 1.50 m/s at yaw 82 - so the thing
to do was measure it, not guess at it. Two new shots steps: 25 takes two pictures half a second apart from hide_22's
bank eye with no car in the water and the current running, 26 the same with `gt.Water.FlowScale 0` set from the runner
through `IConsoleManager`, and `Art/tools/sample_ford.ps1` frame-diffs the water box - the share of its pixels whose
brightest channel moved by more than 12 of 255. **As shipped: 2.76 % flowing against 0.17 % still, mean change 4.18 of
255.** The tell was there and all but invisible: a 0.3 normal tilt on a roughness-0.05 translucent sheet under a flat
sky has almost no specular to swing, which is the same thing section 36 found when the rut water's sky reflection came
to a few dozen pixels.

The fix keeps the normal and puts the same bands into the colour and the opacity through two Multiply nodes, and adds a
third band at 400 cm at full flow speed so the drift reads at a glance instead of as a shimmer. The follow-up's
starting gains, 0.35 on the colour and 0.15 on the opacity, measured **4.39 %** against a 5 % gate - a translucent
sheet's pixel is mostly the bed through it and the sky on it, so swinging the sheet's own albedo buys less than its
size suggests - so they are **0.55 and 0.25**: the colour swings 0.45x to 1.55x, the opacity 0.75x to 1.25x of its

## Water W2: the car floats, the river takes it, the winch brings it back

Stage W2 of the water plan, built on W1's five hull samples: the ones that catch the current also displace water now.
`HullVolumeM3` **3.4** m3 at `SealedFraction` **1.0**, the displacement starting at `HullKeelM` **0.2** m over the
wheels' bottoms (the axle line, so the underbody counts) and full at the old `HullRoofM` 1.9, a fifth of the volume per
sample, `rho x g x V_i` straight up at each sample's own keel point through W1's `AddForceAtLocation` path - no torque
of our own, because putting the lift where the water actually is *is* the righting moment - capped at `FloatCapG`
**1.5** x the weight. The mass is read once at BeginPlay from the mesh's body instance: **2100 kg**, **20.6 kN**, and
every number here is a fraction of it. `gt.Water.Buoyancy` 0 takes the lift off.

**Two numbers were not the plan's.** The volume: where the test parks the car the raised ford stands 1.16 m over the
keel and a 2100 kg car needs **3.13 m3** to float in that, so the plan's 3.0 would have left it sitting on the bed at
0.97 of neutral - and that is the one state whose wheel load cannot be measured, because the traction's load
correction (`Weight / SpringSum`, clamped 0.5 to 5) reads a light spring back up to the full weight until the springs
fall under a fifth of it. 3.4 puts the neutral line at **1.05 m**, nine centimetres clear of the bed. And the damping,
which the plan did not have: W1's drag zeroes its vertical part, so the lift alone is an undamped spring -
19.6 kN/m against 2100 kg is a 2.1 s bob that never dies - so `LiftDampingRatio` **0.5** of critical (6.4 kN per m/s,
shared out by each sample's submerged fraction). The float settles: the keel held **1.02 m** against the predicted
1.05, deepest 1.03.

**The stand-in went and the load did not fall the way the plan assumed.** `WaterBuoyancyGripLoss` 0.4 -> 0, field kept.
In the as-built 0.60 m the lift is already **7,614 N**, 37 % of the weight, and the wheels still read **100.0 %** of it:
the load correction renormalises the springs to the whole weight, so the load only falls near flotation where its 5x
clamp and its 5 %-of-weight cut-off let it (**1.7 %** floating). A wading car's grip, which the stand-in cut by up to
0.4, is now not cut at all. Measured, named, not chased - the traction's constants are the user's.

## Water stage WS1: the simulated surface (VEHICLE_BUILD_SYSTEM 41)

After W2 the user said the water has to "move around like water, like waves", so the plan gained WS1 and WS2 before
W3 and a ford's surface is now simulated rather than levelled. `FGTWaterField` is a shallow-water height field on the
mud field's grid shape, rotated so its X runs along the flow: **120 x 296 = 35,520 cells of 25 cm** over The Hide's
74 x 30 m sheet, the bed sampled from the landscape once in 61 ms, virtual pipes (O'Brien and Hodgins, Mei et al.) in
**1/60 s** sub-steps, at most 4 a frame, **0.570 ms** a stepping frame and **0** clamps. `AGTWaterSheet` hides the
engine cube, lays a **35,520-vertex** procedural grid over the field with its own absolute transform, uploads the
field into a **120 x 296 PF_FloatRGBA** texture every frame (R the surface over the level in cm, G the depth in cm),
and `M_GT_Water` displaces its vertices by R, takes its world normal from that texture's finite differences and masks
its opacity by G. `UGTWaterSubsystem::Sample` answers the field's bilinear surface and the cell's own velocity, so
the wheels, the hull's drag and W2's lift all follow a surface with a shape. W1's constant `FlowAt` and its sine tell
are gone.

**Three of the plan's numbers did not survive contact.** `PipeDamping` 0.02 a step is a linear drag rate of 1.2 per
second, fifty times the bed friction, so it is **1.e-4**; the pipe's constant cross-section makes the steady speed
depend on the cell size instead of the depth, so the acceleration uses the water's own section and the speed is
`sqrt(g h S / Cf)`; and `BedFriction` 0.01 needed half a metre of head for 1.5 m/s, so it is **0.005**. The head is
held **centred** on the level - half over it upstream, half under downstream - so the flow-wise middle, where the
route crosses, stands exactly where W1 and W2 tuned it, and `HeadDropCm` is **8.5**, which reads **1.30 m/s where the
route crosses** (asked 1.5 within 20 %). The gate is the crossing and not the line's mean, because that is what
`FlowSpeedMps` has always meant: at 17 cm of head the line's discharge mean was a tidy 1.87 but the crossing ran
**2.14 m/s** and the terrain test's parked car was taken **2.55 m** against its 0.5 m gate.

**Two bugs the tests found and the plan did not foresee.** A level that jumps cannot be left to the boundaries: 70 cm
put on the two heads alone reaches the middle in 4.8 s but rings the basin's **33 s** seiche, and ten seconds later
the middle sits **38 to 58 cm** off - the first terrain run had a car parked in the ford reading **no water at all**.
So `ShiftLevel` carries the whole surface at once for a change over 5 cm in a tick (**+0.8 cm** off, and the ford
steps settle in 3 s), and the water test still measures the fill from the ends because the plan asked to see it. And
carrying the depth without its fluxes changes the speed - a flux is `v x width x depth` - which left a 1.27 m
channel's fluxes in 0.57 m of water and read **5.42 m/s**.

New test `Art/water_test.bat` and `water` in `Art/suite.sh`, **23 tests** now: the current, the rise, a ring and the
solver's cost, **118 s**, PASS. The ring reaches 5 m in **1.87 s** (asked 2.1 within 0.5), falls 4.92 to 1.71 cm
between 1 and 5 m, conserves its volume to **0.0000 %** and comes back off the 4.8 m bank at **1.81 cm**. The ford
steps read W2's numbers: through at 1145 m in 0.62 m, flooded and stuck in 1.36 m, **0.04 m** downstream held,
**5.73 m** carried at 1.7 % of the weight on the wheels with the keel 3 cm off its predicted line, at the post in
23.1 s - **all six ford gates 1**. `terrain drivetrain grip mud surface track` beside it: five PASS and terrain
FAILing on step 11's stage C exit gate alone, the standing flake.

**Named and not fixed: the water no longer moves at rest.** The motion probe reads **0.17 %** flowing against a 5 %
gate and 0.00 % still, where W1's sine tell read 13.16 %. A steady channel's shallow-water solution is geometrically
steady, and the tell went with the cube; the ring puts **2 crests** into hide_22 1.5 s after an impulse, which is the
proof the surface moves when something moves it. WS2's sources - the car's bow wave and wake, wind, rain - are what
put motion back into still water, and nothing here was patched with a second fake ripple. Not driven, not felt.
