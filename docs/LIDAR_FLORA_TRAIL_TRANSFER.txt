Adaptation
LiDAR uses three source tree shapes with native geometry LODs and eight-triangle
far cards. Their actual meshes, source textures, material coverage, existing wind,
instance placement, collision, minimum LOD and cull budgets are retained.
The source exporters retain authored leaf-mask coordinates in UV0; unique baked
colour atlases use UV1 where required. The transferred fan response uses UV0.

Visible tree/sapling material bindings receive map-local copies. Hidden old
integration trees remain unchanged as recovery components. Near leaves receive
instance-space ellipsoid/fan normals from CrownForm, CrownVolume .5, CrownCard 1,
CrownOcclusion .45 and subsurface colour = final leaf colour * .25. Roughness
is .82 and specular .04. Native Two Sided Foliage was already enabled in LiDAR.
This is an adapted graph, not an identical Blender/Unreal parity assertion.

Near/far foliage share the woodland tint (.76,.86,.94) with restrained per-tree
brightness variation (.88..1.06). The older additional colour-family treatment
is disabled for these copies. Far cards keep their baked normals, shadow field
and Default Lit model; their existing broad crown-normal blend becomes .5.
No new foliage texture samples, meshes, particles or distant geometry are added.

LiDAR's physical sun is 42000 lux with histogram exposure; FloraTrail uses a
different light/exposure/display regime. Copying its raw sun intensity or its
replacement tonemapper would not be a faithful visual migration. LiDAR retains
its sun, skylight, exposure controls, sky, fog and distance profile. Its existing
SDR haze/finish material is copied and extended with the relative grade inside
the same pass: linear-space shadow gamma 1.04, shadow gain (.99,1.01,1.03), and
highlight gain (1.005,1,.99), with smooth luminance masks. The finish decodes and
re-encodes the existing SDR colour; black stays black. Native grading and haze
are not globally replaced, and no additional full-screen pass is introduced.

Bark, ground vegetation, rocks, terrain, mud, water and interactive grass retain
their existing materials. The grade affects their final presentation together.
The previously rejected riverbed experiment is not reinstated. Ground-plant
scale and the outstanding twig/moss-rock complaints are not resolved here.

Review and limits
Evidence: Art/Export/LidarFloraTransfer. The same road, bank, under-canopy and two
valley cameras are used before and after. Normal viewport Shot retains gameplay
LODs. Actual output is 1274x682, with LiDAR's TSR 75% quality profile, rather than
the requested 1600x900 window dimensions. Exposure settings are held; histogram
adaptation can respond to changed foliage luminance. Textures settle before capture.
The camera clips are twelve settled viewpoints over 1.32m, encoded at 3fps. They
are not real-time performance or wind-stability measurements. Source PNGs are
untouched; review boards only arrange/downsample them or convert to greyscale.
The world is paused for each capture, but wind time is not synchronized between
processes. Runtime physics can also move loose branches between captures; these
are not pixel-identical simulation snapshots. Saved geometry is checked separately.

Preserving population, LODs and texture/pass counts does not establish zero GPU
cost. Sustained 60fps and a new physical driving/reset regression remain untested.
FloraTrail's unresolved flat leaf fans and repeated silhouettes do not become
reference-matched merely by transferring them to this larger map.

Visual assessment of the installed candidate
A / palette hierarchy: PARTIAL. Tree greens/olives are more restrained in local
views, but vivid teal shrubs and green fern masses still compete with them;
the woodland reference's repeated coral/litter accents are not established.
B / crown form: PARTIAL. The road and bank have stronger dark/light separation,
but some saplings remain bright cutouts and valley crowns remain repetitive.
The five views do not establish a complete front/side/back-lighting test.
C / readable shade: PARTIAL. Shaded plants retain green/blue-green colour and
the finish lifts some dark detail, but flat fan masses and dark contacts remain.
D / material lighting coherence: FAIL. Bark and road light patches remain clear,
but blue-grey mottled rocks and flat/saturated ground plants are still disconnected
from the painted woodland reference. These materials were not replaced here.
E / depth: PARTIAL. Valley ridges retain progressively cooler, lower contrast
layers; local trunks separate, but the dense understory lacks the reference's
deliberate layered composition. The long valley remains heavily hazed.
F / painterly two-scale read: FAIL at close range. The final sampled-camera
frame shows broad flat leaf sheets and stippling, present in both baseline and
candidate. Shading separation changes, but it does not restore convincing painted
detail. Wider stills are partial. Real-time temporal stability is NOT TESTED;
a settled-camera sequence cannot certify moving wind/TSR quality.
G / consistency: PARTIAL. One profile is used throughout the five-view set,
but the unresolved palette/material/form issues prevent an overall visual pass.
No aggregate score or reference-match claim is made.
