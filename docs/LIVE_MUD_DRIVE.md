# Live clay driving strip

**Since living mud LM5b (2026-10-06) the mud is one layer, the Standard churned bog:** see "The one-layer mud" at the end and `docs/MUD_VARIANTS.md`; the clay notes below describe the mud before it.

## Open and drive

Open `/Game/GrandTour/ArtTests/LiveMudDrive/L_MudDrive`, then Play. The route runs along +X for 140 m: gravel, mud at 32–60 m, and a ford at 88–100 m. The car starts near 5 m. Winch posts stand beside the approaches and exits. This is a material/interaction test strip, not a finished tour leg.

The approved static material studies remain in `L_TussockStyle` and `L_TussockStyle_Overview`. The live shader is shared, so existing maps using `M_GT_Mud` and the Mud/Bog rows of `M_GT_Landscape` receive the approved clay appearance too.

## What changed

- Both ground surfaces sample the approved clay color and normal bakes at the same 6 m scale. Triplanar sampling uses displaced surface height on rut walls.
- Compressed tyre tracks blend toward the smoother worked-clay bake. Raised clay retains the plate detail. Dry roughness is 0.88; wet worked clay reaches 0.48 and darkens.
- The field sampling, displacement, landscape holes, collision, traction, ridge shape and water-table settings are unchanged. The existing water surface and solver handle the ford and exposed rut water.
- Rut-water shading was warmed after a runtime comparison: deep color (0.060, 0.041, 0.025), deep opacity 0.78, roughness 0.12 and grazing roughness 0.08. This changes `M_GT_MudWater` too; the water height and physics are unchanged.
- The new map has its own landscape material, with approved gravel color bakes and a 3 m clay approach blend. Its two patch bounds are specific to this test route; update these masks if the route changes.
- The strip uses 8,380 approved tussock instances with distance culling. Legacy foliage generated by the terrain builder is cleared from this map.
- Three existing C++ methods were exposed to Python/Blueprint for testing: raw steering, test wetness and total displaced-volume measurement. Their implementations did not change.

## Rebuild and checks

From `C:\GrandTour`, with other games and Unreal closed:

```powershell
& 'C:/Program Files/Git/bin/bash.exe' Art/build.sh
& Art/setup_live_mud_drive.ps1
& Art/check_live_mud_drive.ps1
```

The setup is repeatable, but rebuilds this test map and therefore replaces manual edits in it. Run it after legacy `setup_m6_mud` / `setup_m6_hide` material rebuilds, which otherwise restore their old shaders. Do not run those legacy rebuilds merely to open this map.

Before the first material update, the setup saved `BeforeLive_M_GT_Mud`, `BeforeLive_M_GT_Landscape` and `BeforeLive_M_GT_MudWater` under `/Game/GrandTour/ArtTests/MudStyle`. These backups are not overwritten on reruns. The legacy `setup_m6_water` also restores its older water shading; reapply the live setup after it.

The driving check uses the actual car in Play In Editor, crosses both sections dry, then replays the same retained field at controlled 0.8 wetness. It checks completion, lateral deviation, added deformation and wheel water contact. Reports and screenshots are in `Art/Export/LiveMudDrive`; the wet replay records a CSV under `Saved/Profiling/CSV`. Screenshot size is 1440×900; performance uses the actual PIE viewport size recorded in the report, not the screenshot size.

Performance is an editor measurement, not a packaged-game budget or a before/after cost comparison.

Since living mud LM1 (2026-10-05) the check plays in a new 1440x900 window (client 1440x868) and, in that one session, drives the dry crossing with `gt.Mud.Settle 0`, again on reset fields with `gt.Mud.Settle 1` (the settle cost readout), the settle pair on a fresh rut 4.5 m to the side (`Mud_Settle_0s.png`, `Mud_Settle_5s.png`, `settle_profile.csv`), the pictures above, the wet replay and a last dry crossing with `gt.Mud.Settle 0`; each crossing is profiled. It takes about 7 minutes. The wrapper judges by a fresh `ok: true` report; the editor's -1073741819 exit is printed as the known shutdown fault.

## Validation

- Build: `Art/build.sh` passed (exit 0).
- Full `Art/suite.sh`: 26 PASS / 2 FAIL. The known winch-anchor timeout passed its one prescribed rerun. The remaining failure is terrain step 11's marginal 30-degree rut-exit timing comparison (about 5.7 s versus 5.4 s along the rut; the test uses unrounded values and a ratio threshold). Physics was not tuned to change this result. The terrain test's deformation, rut-water, ford and flotation/winch checks passed.
- Full suite output: `Art/Export/LiveMudDrive/suite.txt`; winch rerun: `winch-rerun.txt`. These are headless regression checks; the separate PIE drive provides the rendered check.
- Final rendered dry/wet driving check and launcher: PASS. Dry completed at 119.03 m, adding 12.44 m³ of deformation; wet completed at 119.05 m, adding another 7.53 m³ to the retained field. Maximum lateral deviation 0.36 m; maximum wheel-water depths 0.550 / 0.490 m. These volumes are the existing field's integrated lowered volume, not a new soil-conservation claim.
- Final wet replay: 9.526 ms median / 11.903 ms p95 GPU time over 3,401 frames, at a 1478×592 PIE viewport. Frame time was capped near 16.667 ms. The RTX 4050 was under software thermal slowdown (84 C at the end; 86 C in an earlier run). No added-cost or 60-fps packaged-game claim is made. Source: `Saved/Profiling/CSV/Profile(20260929_160134).csv`. This was before the final water tint defaults were saved; the tint comparison was captured in that same session after profiling.

## Visual inspection and remaining limits

Inspected cockpit gravel/mud/ford views and dry/wet rut close-ups. The approved clay plate detail remains on untouched ground; compressed rut floors use smoother detail. The water-hidden diagnostic shows that the broad nearly black strips were predominantly the older rut-water material, not missing clay: max RGB below 40 in the fixed measured region fell from 14.418% to 0.078% when rut water was hidden. The refined-water trial measured 0.084% and was selected, then saved to the master with a successful material compile. Small dark creases remain in the existing rut grid.

The diagnostic screenshots were taken sequentially while wetness decayed (about 0.74, 0.62, 0.50 respectively); therefore these percentages are diagnostic evidence, not a controlled same-wetness color delta. The harness now holds test wetness during inspection as well as driving for future captures. No repeat physics suite was needed for the final shading-only tint defaults.

`Mud_PuddleTint_Candidate.png` shows the runtime trial whose parameter values are now saved. `Mud_After_Wet.png` retains the earlier darker water for comparison. `Art/tools/sample_live_mud_water.ps1` reproduces the pixel counts. This is a functional strip, with simple hills and incomplete distant dressing; it is not a finished regional scene. User review of the live art and driving feel is still pending.

## The one-layer mud (living mud LM5b, 2026-10-06)

Since LM5b (`docs/LIVING_MUD_PLAN.md` LM5b, `docs/MUD_VARIANTS.md`) the strip's mud section is a **Standard** bog
(`FGTRouteSection::MudVariant`'s default) and it wears the **Standard churned-bog bake everywhere in its patch** - untouched
mud, ruts, ridges, banks and the clods alike. The clay and worked-clay layers above are gone from the mud mesh; the clay stays
on the landscape outside the mud. The feel is unchanged (Standard has no profile; the field and the traction read exactly as
before, the mud CRC 8e0aad06).

`M_GT_Mud` (`Art/Unreal/live_mud_style.py`), the `Mud surface (PS)` block:
- **One layer:** the patch's slice (`MudVariant`, pushed by `AGTMudPatch`; 0 Standard) of the four texture arrays
  `TA_MS_Bog{Color,Normal,Rough,Height}` (`Art/Unreal/import_mud_variants.py`), tri-planar at the clay's weights
  (pow 6). The bake's own roughness (Standard's slurry 0.22-0.38, lump tops 0.83) is the mud's roughness.
- **Two tilings that share no repeat** on the top face: `BogTileCm` 300 square and `BogTile2Cm` 430 turned `BogTile2Deg` 37,
  height-blended (the higher bake wins over `BogBlendSharp` 0.2 of the height, so the mix never averages two patterns into a
  smoother one) by two octaves of world value noise at `BogMixCm` 260. The walls take one tiling each. The uvs are not wrapped
  in the shader (a frac would put a mip seam on every tile edge); the samples take explicit gradients.
- **A rut differs only by** its shape (the field's normal and the triplanar walls), the **fresh sheen** (`FreshWet` 0.3, was
  0.6: the field's Fresh darkens by up to 15 % x 0.3 and glosses toward 0.5, only ever glossier, drying as Fresh fades over
  `FreshFadeS`), the **weather's wetness** (as before: 18 % darker, 35 % in the tyres' compressed ground, which glosses toward
  0.48) and the **rut water** (`M_GT_MudWater`, unchanged). Gone: the worked-clay bake in the tracks, the matte crests
  (roughness 0.88 on raised mud) and the Bog row's 15 % darkening.
- **The far gloss fade** (`FarGlossFade`, a static switch, on): the roughness rises toward `FarGlossRough` 0.65 (only where
  it is below it) between `FarGlossNearCm` 600 and `FarGlossFarCm` 1200 from the camera, A2's candidate for the sky's
  reflection banding across far glossy ruts.

The landscape (`M_GT_Landscape`'s mud block, and this strip's `M_LiveLandscape`, now built by
`live_mud_style.live_landscape`): outside the mud the clay as before. Round each live patch's box (`MPC_TrackPaint`
`Patch0..3`, written at BeginPlay) the patch's slice (`PatchVariant0..3`, beside the boxes) over a 300 cm apron, fading to
the clay (the Mud rows) or the gravel (this strip's road), colour, normal and roughness alike, with the same far fade. Until
LM5b the strip's apron was the clay inside two boxes measured on this strip; it now follows the live boxes, so it exists only
in play (as the hole always has). The clods (`M_GT_Clod`) take the bog arrays' colour and normal at their old 150 cm scale,
the slice from their patch (`MudVariant`).

Not checked on this strip: no drive check or picture of L_MudDrive ran in LM5b (the four variants were looked at on
`L_MudVariants`; see `docs/MUD_VARIANTS.md`).
