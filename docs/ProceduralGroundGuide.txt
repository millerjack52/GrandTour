Live wheel-mud buildup — 16 October 2026
The native visual path now runs a separate Push stage after excavation. It
raises yielding shoulders, writes lateral movement, and lets subsequent tyres
remould those shoulders. The previous native branch only queued cuts and
bypassed the old deposit code. Both raised and lowered cells notify geometry
consumers. Idle frames do not grow piles; overlapping stamps cannot reuse the
same excavation budget.

This applies to the existing playable Road 01 soft/churned/deep sections.

Living road update — 16 October 2026
The detailed road tiles now enter the vehicle's soil-contact path. Previously,
that path accepted only the original Landscape actor, even though the surface
query already accepted the tiles. Immersion now uses the detailed road's upper
height rather than the Landscape recessed beneath it. This restores the existing
native-derived substance response on generated roads; it does not replace Chaos
with Havok or establish full native vehicle/body displacement parity.

Firmness now also controls physical softness (1 - Firmness). Wetness remains a
separate appearance input. Grass is visual cover over the same yielding soil.
The native extrusion and visual mud-update equations remain in use.

Road > Conditions has four additional host authoring controls:
  Track Spread Cm: alternate traffic lines fan out and rejoin the main tracks.
  Churn: broad disturbed patches between wheel channels, from 0 to 1.
  Crossing Tracks: intermittent crossing channels, from 0 to 1.
  Breakup Cm: small clumps/depressions in the authored road bed.
These compose the initial road. They are not wheel-generated live deformation,
and are not claimed to reproduce a recovered native procedural generator.

The existing four Road 01 sections now demonstrate firm dirt, yielding grassy
ground, a wider churned crossing, and deeper soft mud with depression-filled
water. Their maximum soil-layer inputs are 2 / 42 / 40 / 90cm; actual depth is
spatially varied and actual wheel sink depends on load, soil state and support.
The 90cm input does NOT mean the car automatically sinks 90cm.
The authored rut-depth controls are 3 / 8 / 22 / 35cm respectively.
Use Rebuild then Save All after editing. No extra rock scatter was added.

Road conditions pilot — 16 October 2026
Select Road 01 - Conditions: firm grass shallow deep in the existing authoring
map. In Details, expand Road > Conditions. The first 180m contains four editable
examples: Firm dirt (0-45m), Worn grassy track (45-90m), Shallow mud (90-135m),
and Deep mud and rut pools (135-180m). Each has a four-metre end blend.
Distances follow the recovered road's control-polygon knot distance, which can
differ slightly from the final curved centreline's arc length.

Start/End Metres move a stretch along the road. Blend Metres softens its ends.
Mud Depth Cm supplies the existing physical soil model; it does not carve the
initial road. Rut Depth Cm controls initial geometric grooves and shoulders.
Firmness and Wetness are independent inputs to the existing response model,
not new native Havok constants. Grass Cover changes the surface coverage mask.
Water Fill Cm is a maximum above a depression's lowest bed, also limited by its
spill point. It does not guarantee water on a slope. Zero disables filling.
Detail Seed changes the correlated rut breakup and wandering, not the spline.
Overlapping Conditions apply in array order; later entries blend over earlier
soil values and add their relief. Prefer non-overlapping ranges initially.

Press Rebuild on the road after edits, then Save All. Empty roads have an Add
Example Conditions button; it never replaces an existing Conditions array.
These host procedural rules build separate relief, soil and water layers based
on the Island findings. They are not a recovered native automatic road generator.

Generated pools survey the final road collision bed at 25cm, fill enclosed
depressions and clip water triangles to that bed. No new ellipse is carved.
Pools share the existing water sheet's moving simulation and visual effects.
The existing solver remains 62.5cm, so very narrow puddles have limited dynamic
resolution. Existing map puddles/rivers remain separately authored; this pilot
does not replace all earlier oval puddles throughout Big Mountain.

Fine soil values are saved with each road tile so adjacent ruts can have
different depth/firmness despite the coarse whole-map paint atlas. The existing
surface actor's Maximum Layer Depth Cm still caps physical depth. The runtime
model and its limits have not been replaced with a claimed native equivalent.
The road's visual grass/material masks still use the existing map-wide atlas.

Clear Generated restores the captured terrain/masks and original water domain,
and removes this controller's road pool mesh. Rebuild reinstates the controls.
The old water-bed protection now fades continuously instead of switching at a
boolean shoreline. The controller owns only its generated pool data and mesh;
the original water data asset is retained unchanged.

Road clutter correction — 16 October 2026
The initial layered preset overpopulated the carriageway with large clods.
MudChunks' native candidate budget was not a suitable final density: the native
reference dirt distribution averages only .03024 red-channel density, with
coverage above the brush threshold across about 29.5% of the reference. Our
road-wide generated mask was substantially different.
The study preset now uses sparse, smaller verge clods: budget 64, density .3,
30cm road clearance, 350cm verge band, cluster strength .95, size ranges
.55–1.1 and .5–.9 (plus native jitter). These are host composition choices,
not claimed recovered native values. Road relief and other layers are retained.
The correction is also in the Density preset, so rebuilding does not restore
the excessive road scatter. Art/procedural_ground.ps1 -Mode ClodCorrection
applies only this rule to an existing study and verifies other placements,
terrain, relief-tile retention, repeatability, save/reload and protected assets.
Its report is clodcorrection-functional.json with a separate process report;
this headless correction does not certify rendered appearance. The previous
study is backed up in Art/Export/ProceduralGround/BeforeClodCorrection.

Edit a road
1. Select "Road 01 - edit control points then Rebuild" in the Outliner.
2. Select RoadControlPoints and move/add its spline control points in the viewport.
3. Point Scale Y changes width locally. Width Cm sets the overall width.
4. Project Points To Base places control points on the captured terrain.
5. Press Rebuild on the road or Ground Authoring actor. Save All afterward.
Duplicate a road or region to add another. Its Author property must point to the
same Ground Authoring controller, and it must lie inside Generation Bounds.
The yellow preview after rebuild is the reconstructed natural cubic. Unreal's
normal spline widget uses a different interpolation; the generated result uses
our reconstructed evaluator, not the widget's Hermite curve.

Edit vegetation and soil
Select the Woodland or Wet ground region. Move/rotate it and edit its Bounds
extent. Grass Cover, Wetness and Mud Depth control appearance/response; regions
do not turn a grass-covered soil into physically firm ground automatically.
Region shapes and ecology variation are our authoring rules, not claimed native
algorithm parity. Exclusion regions protect terrain and suppress generated
plants. Clearing regions reduce woody cover. Region order is stable actor-name
order; overlapping authored masks blend in that order.

Select Ground Authoring to change Seed or Scatter Rules. Rules expose meshes,
candidate budget, density, size range, road clearance, slope limit and culling.
The recovered candidate generator uses a budget/4 count per 8m candidate square,
16 pattern variants, and inverse-distance repulsion rather than hard spacing.
Road debris uses solid collision only when a mesh has prepared simple collision.
Changing a seed changes the generated arrangement, not the source terrain.

Recovery and ownership
Capture Base is already done for this study. Rebuild always reads that immutable
snapshot, preventing old trenches from accumulating. Clear Generated restores
terrain and masks to that capture and removes only this controller's scatter.
It retains the road/region controls and snapshot, so Rebuild restores the result.
Capture Base refuses to overwrite an existing snapshot.
Use one controller per landscape. The first version requires a single landscape
edit layer, as this study uses. Do not hand-edit its three owned output masks;
they are generated products. Terrain edits inside its generation area would be
replaced by the next rebuild. Unrelated actors and original map assets are not
owned by the tool. Old one-off scatter inside this separate study was removed
before capture; Clear does not reconstruct that older placement system.
Use Clear Generated before deleting the controller; its output actors are
separate actors so they remain available in the playable level.

Current boundaries
- First bounded authoring section, up to 1024m across. Not whole-world streaming.
- Actual placement math runs in Unreal C++; no native STG/map output is imported.
- Broad landscape sculpting respects the existing approximately 5m vertex grid.
  Existing near-player mud rendering, deformation and grass remain active.
  Fine road relief now uses local mesh tiles with matching triangle collision.
- Existing river/pond assets and simulation are retained. Road shaping protects
  sampled wet ground. New pond/river generation is not part of this first tool.
- Generated 64m HISM cells are retained when their instance output is unchanged.
  Input evaluation and the small region's masks are still recomputed on Rebuild;
  this is not yet a fully incremental dependency graph or streaming scheduler.
- Native spline, candidate, density, scale and end-fade kernels have oracle tests.
  Local seeds, mask sampling, ecology, slope rejection, endpoint caps, width
  parameterization and mesh adapters do not establish full editor parity.
- Detailed terrain, foliage art and long-distance performance need further work.

Fine road relief
The existing authoring study now includes a dirt-road relief profile. Select a
Road actor and expand Fine relief. Apply Relief enables its height texture;
Relief Strength scales the signed offset (1 uses the recovered native amplitude).
Set strength to 0 to compare against the same road bed without that offset.
Rebuild regenerates geometry, collision and scatter roots together. Save All
saves the tiles inside the level and the owned surface masks.

Profiles are in /Game/GrandTour/ArtTests/ProceduralGround/RoadProfiles:
  T_dirt_road   Default Width 700cm   Diffuse Aspect 2
  T_grass_road  Default Width 400cm   Diffuse Aspect 2
  T_dirt_path   Default Width 600cm   Diffuse Aspect 2
When selecting another texture, set its matching default width/aspect above.
Default Width controls texture coordinates; the road's Width Cm and point Scale Y
still control the actual road footprint. These are separate native controls.
These presets select relief only: surface colour, grass cover and soil wetness
remain controlled by the existing road/region inputs.

Ground Authoring exposes Relief Spacing Cm (25cm in this study), Relief Material,
triangle counts and changed/retained tile counts. Tiles align to four landscape
cells, taper at their outer boundary, and share the same samples for rendering
and collision. Generation is rejected above 1.2 million vertices before edits.
The study currently has 53 tiles / 678,400 triangles. These are conventional
procedural meshes with frustum culling, not Nanite or a finished streaming/LOD
solution. They remain loaded with the study map; budget larger road networks
before extending this to the entire mountain. A natural cubic road can change
along its full length after moving one point, requiring many tiles to rebuild.

The coarse landscape is recessed under the tiles to keep its flat collision
out of the new hollows. Never delete tile actors by hand: use Clear Generated
to restore the captured landscape and remove owned tiles/scatter together.
Terrain queries, visible mud, tread strips, grass roots and water bed queries
use the detailed base height. Existing live mud deformation is added afterward.
The texture offsets are centimetre-scale solid relief, not a substitute for
the separate deep authored mud layer or a change to mud softness.
