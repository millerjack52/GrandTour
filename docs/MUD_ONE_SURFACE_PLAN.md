## 1. What is there now (2026-09-15, stages A-C plus the hole)

- **The field** `FGTMudField` (`Source/GrandTour/Terrain/GTMudField.h/.cpp`): a 12.5 cm grid per bog
  (`UGTMudSubsystem::CellCm`) with, per cell, the mud height in metres (negative in a rut, positive on a ridge), a
  slide-away offset XY in metres (kept, unused for displacement) and a tracks factor 0..1 (1 under any wheel).
  `StampWheel` lowers a capsule under a tyre and returns `RidgeReturn` of the volume as ridges within `RidgeWidthCm`;
  `StampBelly` flattens under the chassis. The traction stamps them and reads the field inside a patch (stage C).
- **The patch** `AGTMudPatch` (`GTMudPatch.h/.cpp`): one per Mud route section, spawned by `UGTMudSubsystem::
  BuildPatches` at BeginPlay over the section's footprint plus `MarginCm`. `Texture` (PF_FloatRGBA: R height m, GB
  offset m, A tracks) is re-uploaded per dirty rect each tick the field changes; `LayerTexture` (PF_G8, 1 where the
  landscape's Bog layer is, 0 where Mud) is baked once by `BakeLayers`. `Mesh` is a `UProceduralMeshComponent` grid at
  `MeshCellCm` 12.5 over the box, each vertex at the landscape's height (`ALandscapeProxy::GetHeightAtLocation`) plus
  `MeshLiftCm` 2, no collision, no shadow; it wears `MeshMaterial`, a dynamic instance of `M_GT_Mud` with
  PatchMin/PatchSize/PatchTexels/MudField/MudLayer. The Hide's patch: 555 x 286 vertices, 315,780 triangles, one draw.
- **The mud material** `M_GT_Mud` (`Art/Unreal/setup_m6_mud.py`, `Art/setup_m6_mud.bat`): masked, one-sided,
  world-space normal. `WPO_HLSL` displaces the vertex by the field's height. `SURFACE_HLSL` derives the normal by finite
  difference, takes colour and roughness from `GroundColor`/`BogColor`/`GroundRoughness`/`BogRoughness` by the baked
  layer (the landscape's Mud and Bog rows: 0.28 0.20 0.12 / 0.55 and 0.18 0.13 0.08 / 0.45), churns by `TrackDarken`
  0.85 where tracked, darkens and glosses with `Wetness` (MPC_Weather) and the rut depth, and masks the pixel out
  where the field is untouched (`tracks > 0.02 || abs(height) > 0.005`). `MudDiagnostic` switches a colour ramp on
  (R height, G ground Z window, B tracks) for the 40 m picture. The script also adds `Patch0..3` and `PatchCount` to
  `MPC_TrackPaint`.
- **The landscape material** `M_GT_Landscape` (`build_landscape_material` in `Art/Unreal/setup_m6_hide.py`): a copy of
  `M_GT_Environment` (MF_Weather: wetness, puddles, the paint ruts outside bogs) with Landscape Layer Blend nodes for
  BaseColor, Roughness and Porosity over the `LAYERS` table (Tarmac, Dirt, Mud, Snow, Scrub, Bog: constant colour,
  roughness, porosity per row; no textures, no normal). Masked since the hole: `MUD_HOLE_HLSL` finds which patch box
  the pixel is in, samples that patch's field texture (`MudField0..3`, texture object parameters defaulting to the
  engine's Black) and returns 0 where the mesh's own condition holds. The field textures reach the landscape through
  its dynamic material instances: `BuildLegLandscape` (`GTVehicleSetupLibrary.cpp`) sets `bUseDynamicMaterialInstance`
  before `Import`, and `UGTMudSubsystem::BuildPatches` calls `SetLandscapeMaterialTextureParameterValue` per patch,
  then writes the boxes into `MPC_TrackPaint`. A material may reference at most two parameter collections; the
  landscape has Weather and TrackPaint.
- **Pictures and samplers**: `hide_shots.bat` step 12 crawls the bog and shoots it from 40 m with the diagnostic ramp
  (`sample_mud_diag.ps1` samples the logged pixels); step 13 crawls a second line 35 cm beside the first and takes
  four eye-level pictures of the band (`hide_13_rut_low` as shipped, 14 slide on, 15 mesh hidden, 16 landscape
  shadow off) and logs the band's screen box (`rutbox`); `sample_rut_low.ps1` counts, inside it, the pixels whose
  brightest channel is under 40, with a six-band histogram. After the hole: 12 dark of 28,480 in hide_13 (1,071 of
  85,440 before it). Picture 15 now shows the landscape's holes and means nothing.
- **What the user sees**: the ruts read as cut into the ground, but a thin dark line runs along the deformed region's
  near edges: the boundary between touched and untouched cells, where the mesh (2 cm up) meets the hole's edge, and a
  ray under the mesh's edge lands inside the hole on nothing. That boundary is the fault; Spintires does not have one.

## 2. What Spintires does, as far as its author has described it publicly

Take this as the design target, not as its code. In a mud area the ground is the deformable mesh itself: the terrain
is tessellated on the GPU and displaced by a deformation texture, and shaded from the same texture, so there is one
surface and nothing to seam. The deformation texture carries the mud height and a churn state; the tyre tread is
stamped into it so the rut floor carries the lug pattern; the mud is textured (wet and glossy in the ruts, drier on
the ridges); water fills the ruts below the area's water level; the physics reads the same field (we have that).
