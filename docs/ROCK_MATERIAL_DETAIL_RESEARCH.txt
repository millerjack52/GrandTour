ROCK DETAIL WITHOUT ADDITIONAL GEOMETRY
8 November 2026. Research and proposed next pass; no asset or shader changes.

Recommendation
Keep the six outcrops at their current 2,112 triangles. Author a coherent rock
surface with readable weathered faces and mineral patches, then combine its
baked normal with a smaller repeating grain normal. Improve matching colour,
roughness and moss masks before evaluating parallax. This is a specific follow-up
to LIDAR_PERCEIVED_DETAIL_RESEARCH.txt, based on the rejected rock preview.

Why the current study still looks plain
Art/tools/bake_rock_weathering.py builds smooth noise, Gaussian pits and bowls.
Art/Blender/build_forest_floor_revision.py:weathered_rock_material adds separate
colour noise and fine bump. These produce grain, but little coherent chipped or
fractured structure. This is an artistic diagnosis, not a measured GPU result.
The packed RAH image contains roughness, cavity and height; only roughness is
currently connected. Colour and surface shape largely describe different noise.
Simply increasing normal strength or texture resolution will retain that pattern.

Technique choices
1. Bake surface structure onto the existing mesh. Epic documents baking high-poly
   surface normals into textures, and Adobe documents baked maps as inputs to
   material masks [1,2]. A detailed offline sculpt can supply worn fracture faces,
   shallow chips and hollows without shipping its additional polygons. Author
   matching pigment and roughness from the same feature masks; pigment stains
   must not automatically become height. Keep directional lighting out of colour.
2. Layer detail at different scales. Epic's detail-texturing workflow combines
   base and repeating detail textures [3]. Proposed art scales, to validate:
   30-100 cm mineral/weathering patches, 2-20 cm erosion and worn fracture detail,
   then millimetre-scale grain. Keep the existing rounded silhouettes. The middle
   scale should read at walking distance; tiny grain is for nearby inspection.
3. Compose normals correctly. Use a tested reoriented/angle-corrected normal
   blend, not colour-style addition or averaging. The original RNM article shows
   why naive combinations flatten the base orientation and detail [4]. Verify
   tangent basis and green-channel orientation when moving the +Y Blender maps
   into Unreal. Keep normal and packed data textures linear, not colour-corrected.
4. Give moss texture and placement logic. Proposal: combine the existing basal
   vertex mask with texture-scale cavities and broken coverage. Use a separate
   moss normal and roughness response, leaving substantial exposed mineral above.
   Bake complex mask generation offline where practical. Curvature is a placement
   aid, not an instruction to make every edge green or white.
5. Optional parallax. BumpOffset shifts texture coordinates from a height map,
   giving camera-dependent depth without additional polygons [5]. Compare it
   only after the normal-only surface is convincing. Iterative POM/relief mapping
   adds ray/height intersection work and sampling trade-offs [6]. Trial a bounded
   near-view version if needed; do not apply it throughout the forest by default.
   Standard normal/BumpOffset materials do not reshape the rock outline. No
   tessellation or vertex displacement is proposed for this pass.

Concrete next experiment
A. Freeze mesh positions/topology, camera, lighting, placement and exposure.
B. Build one reference-led rock material set: base colour, structural normal,
   packed roughness/cavity/height, and a shared fine-detail normal. Use the owner's
   Waimeamea rocky-shallows photograph for character; do not infer exact geology.
   Start at the existing 1K structural-map size and evaluate feature readability
   before increasing resolution. Correct UV stretching on steep rock sides if
   necessary; document UV edits and any resulting render-vertex splits separately.
C. Compare current versus new material on the same outcrop: neutral-grey normal
   view, colour-only view, roughness view, then combined shading. Review close up,
   at walking distance and across the bank. Require coherent weathered structure,
   distinct mineral faces, textured irregular basal moss and no uniform crack grid.
D. Test slow camera movement in Unreal at the saved 1600x900/TSR75 preset. Check
   mip transitions, grain shimmer, stretching and normal seams. Filter/fade tiny
   detail that cannot remain stable; Epic describes this aliasing concern [1].
E. Only then compare normal-only, BumpOffset and an optional short-step POM variant.
   Judge motion and grazing angles. Fading an effect visually does not prove its
   samples stopped executing: verify shader compilation and actual GPU cost.

Performance and delivery constraints
No additional triangles does not mean no additional rendering cost. Share maps
and material instances across the rocks, pack scalar maps when appropriate, and
avoid multiplying every layer through three-axis projection. Keep normals in a
normal-suitable format. Baked masks can avoid complex runtime procedural work.
Texture memory, sampling and POM costs require measurement; no speedup is claimed.
Performance benchmarking remains pending at the user's request while Skate shares
the GPU. The current forest study is still a Blender prototype, not an installed
Unreal improvement. Before promotion, verify contacts, exported masks and material
appearance in game. This research changes no meshes, materials or textures.
