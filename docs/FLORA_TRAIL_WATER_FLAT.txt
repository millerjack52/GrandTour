FloraTrail: level standing water
===============================

Scope
The MudFlat candidate retains the accepted Brown Mud wet material and uses a
separate, map-local water material. Its water mesh sits at FieldLevelCm with
zero vertex displacement and an upward surface normal. The deformable mud bed,
source ground textures, tyre material, water solver, sediment and foam inputs
remain unchanged. LiDAR and the source-calibrated pine diagnostic are protected.

Cause and controlled comparison
The previous water material added physical-field elevation and a native ripple
term to vertex position. Its normals combined an optical wave atlas with the
gradients of both fields. On shallow standing water these created broad rolling
highlights. Setting displacement alone to zero did not remove that appearance:
the geometry-only control still looked wavy. Removing the wave normals as well
produced level puddles and a calm pond in the fixed-camera comparison.

FloraWaterFlat=1 removes both displacement terms. The same control removes the
height offsets from the depth calculation, so clipping and absorption refer to
the level surface. Physical wet coverage still limits the water footprint.
FloraWaterQuietNormals=1 gives the surface an upward normal. Both are saved
defaults. The baseline control sets both to zero; geometry_only uses (1,0).
This is a standing-water appearance profile, not a general river material.

The visible surface intentionally does not rise into simulated waves or wake
crests. Physical water depth, wheel interaction, mud deformation and native
sediment/foam simulation still run. Removing visible wave crests is part of
the requested flat appearance, not a claim that the simulation was flattened.

Reproduction
With other Unreal/Blender renderers closed, run sequentially from C:/GrandTour:
  powershell -File Art/run_flora_water_flat.ps1 -Stage probe
  powershell -File Art/run_flora_water_flat.ps1 -Stage verify
  python Art/review_flora_water_flat.py

Probe clones L_FloraTrail_MudWet into L_FloraTrail_MudFlat and clones the local
M_FloraWater into MudTrial/M_WaterFlat. No old package is overwritten. It uses
the existing mud harness with fixed exposure, camera, lighting and render
settings, waits for source texture loading, pauses simulation for comparison,
then captures six views and a sampled short motion check per control.
Verify reloads the saved map, checks the live defaults and mesh plane, captures
again, and runs the existing continuous driving test. It must leave every
MudTrial package unchanged and exit zero with no material compilation errors.

Evidence
Art/Export/FloraTrail/WaterFlat contains native Unreal frames, comparison boards,
320px colour/greyscale thumbnails, sampled motion, reports and process logs.
The images render at the recorded native backbuffer size, not the requested
window size. Motion is sampled evidence, not a sustained-framerate benchmark.
Docs/validation/flora-trail-water-flat.json records actual measured results.

Recovery and limits
Art/flora_water_flat_baseline.py snapshots all 41 existing MudTrial packages
to D:/GrandTourAssets/Backups and verifies them along with the previous baseline
chain. The former MudWet map and original water material remain recoverable.
Drive-FloraTrail-Mud-Waves.bat opens that former version.
Drive-FloraTrail-Mud.bat opens the flat-water candidate after verification.
The bank/shore transition is still visibly hard in places. This change does not
establish a reference-matched forest palette or sustained 60 fps. The broader
A-G palette gate status remains explicitly separate in the validation record.
