LiDAR visual-preserving 60 fps optimization plan
==============================================
19 November 2026. Requested after the fine-grass restoration.
First driving measurements and ranked costs are recorded in
LIDAR_DRIVING_BASELINE_2026-11-19.md.

Outcome and baseline
Aim for sustained 60 fps on the actual target laptop without reducing the chosen
visual quality. Current detected CPU/GPU: Ryzen 7 8845HS and RTX 4050 Laptop GPU.
The existing v7 profile targets 1600x900, TSR 75%, dynamic resolution off, the
current authored tree LODs, virtual shadows and native mud/water/grass interaction.
Confirm actual viewport, GPU/VRAM, driver, power/thermal state and runtime settings
at the start. This is not a native-1080p claim. A different resolution is a separate
target, never an undeclared way to pass.

Freeze the saved grass-restoration map, material settings, normal launcher,
vehicle and graphics profile as a recoverable comparison baseline. Its unresolved
tree colour, pale shaded verges and decorative ground-plant issues remain listed;
"baseline" is not a claim that the owner has approved every visual element.
Keep further art changes separate from optimization comparisons. Rebase measured
results if the selected visual baseline changes.

Acceptance gates
- Rendering budget: 16.667 ms/frame. Report mean, median, p95, p99, maximum,
  percentage over budget, and hitches over 33.3/50/100ms, separately for each route
  segment. A fast valley must not average away a slow forest or water crossing.
- Initial sustained-work target: p95 at or below 16.667ms in every representative
  segment, then reduce p99 excursions and recurring hitches before acceptance.
  The final report must show the tail behavior even if the p95 target passes;
  p95 alone does not establish a locked 60 fps experience.
- Aim for roughly 2ms of GPU/limiting-thread headroom in steady heavy views so
  normal gameplay bursts do not immediately break the budget. Measure the actual
  CPU/GPU bottleneck; do not add pipelined CPU and GPU durations together.
- Run uncapped for work/headroom measurement, then check real foreground gameplay
  at the normal 60fps presentation target. Offscreen CSV results alone do not prove
  smooth display pacing, input latency or a sustained driving result.
- No visual regression in matched native colour/greyscale views and real-time
  camera/drive clips: density, silhouettes, contact, source detail, wind, lighting,
  atmospheric layers and near/far transitions must survive.
- Native wheel contact, grass bending/removal, deformation, water and vehicle
  handling remain correct. Keep audio and normal gameplay work enabled for final
  acceptance; a muted diagnostic is not the complete game budget.

1. Repair the measurement workflow before collecting a baseline
Reuse Art/profile_lidar_play.ps1, native gt.Perf instrumentation and
Art/tools/analyze_lidar_play_perf.py, but do not run the old harness unchanged:
- Its default Art/Export/LidarStudy/Place fixtures are currently unavailable on
  C:. Make new C-owned fixtures from the installed map and known review cameras.
- It schedules HighResShot. That forces LOD0 in this engine and is unsuitable
  for gameplay visual comparison. Use normal Shot outside measurement windows.
- It records requested resolution, not a verified runtime viewport. Reject a
  benchmark with unexpected output/internal resolution; the recent PIE review
  demonstrated 1274x682 vs 1600x868 windows despite identical requested sizes.
- Keep logical frame-boundary CSV timing, but report Render/RHI thread columns
  where available as well as game/GPU work. Distinguish raw frame work from cap
  waits/presentation. Replace the historical 16.9ms tolerance with explicit
  16.667ms reporting for this target.
- Frame-count throttle/teleport segments are useful probes but not an identical
  continuous route when frame rate changes. Use time/distance route checkpoints,
  record actual trajectory/speed, and separate stationary probes from route tests.
- Add the current single-renderer reservation, disk/memory guard, bounded timeout,
  correct-exit checks and C-only evidence paths. Do not terminate unrelated apps.

2. Establish representative standalone measurements
Run the saved map in standalone game mode, outside the editor, at the declared
quality. Start with a warm-up sufficient for shaders, textures, grass and temporal
history; verify readiness rather than trusting a fixed short delay. Use normal
shipping-like streaming, not the visual-review force-all-used-textures setting.
Collect at least three repeated warm runs, alternating baseline/candidate order
when comparing. Record cold traversal separately. Keep real driving hitches in
the route statistics; do not discard slow frames as "warm-up" after the drive starts.

Coverage must include:
 a) Fine-grass paddock low view and fast traversal through the near/far boundary.
 b) Forest entry and dense under-canopy path, including turning toward the sun.
 c) Mixed-material bank: leaf/fern/bark/rock/road together, close to source detail.
 d) Mud braking/acceleration and repeated tyre tracks with native deformation.
 e) Water crossing with ripples, wet surfaces and the normal vehicle effects.
 f) Valley overlook plus approach/turn, exposing distant canopy and fog cost.
 g) At least one continuous multi-minute drive linking the demanding sections,
    repeated after enough play to reveal accumulated contacts/particles/streaming.
Use a normal vehicle, camera, weather/time and normal gameplay/audio settings.

3. Find the real limiting work
Record game, render/RHI and GPU timings; draw/primitive/triangle counts; memory,
texture mip/residency state; streaming and garbage-collection spikes. Correlate
the slow frames with native gt.Perf scopes, CSV GPU passes and focused Unreal
Insights/GPU captures. Collect expensive diagnostic traces separately to quantify
their overhead. Rank bottlenecks in milliseconds, not by how complex an asset looks.
Temporary disable-one-system probes may identify cost, but are not candidate
optimizations and must never be presented as visual-preserving improvements.

4. Optimize avoidable work first, in measured priority order
CPU/streaming:
- Inspect grass ring updates, repeated scans/allocations, dirty-state propagation,
  vegetation bounds/registration, terrain queries and contact bookkeeping.
- Inspect mud/soil/water uploads, copies, synchronization and update scheduling.
  Retain visible response and simulation accuracy; do not simply lower physics rate.
- Inspect unnecessary tick/duplicate controllers, disabled/hidden components that
  still do work, resource churn and garbage collection. Prove the removed work was
  unused, while retaining hidden recovery data in recoverable assets where needed.
- Keep vehicle physics, panel/contact behavior and audio in the budget. Optimize
  implementation or scheduling, not gameplay fidelity.

GPU/visibility:
- Measure actual fine-grass vertex cost versus masked overdraw. Restored near
  grass is 144/64/36 triangles; do not assume either geometry or pixel work dominates.
- Inspect virtual-shadow page invalidation from foliage wind, shadow caster
  bounds and needless distant updates. Preserve visible moving leaf shadows.
- Check instance spatial batching/culling and LOD transitions using live gameplay
  views. Preserve root positions and stable colour/random seeds when rebatching.
- Audit expensive material paths, duplicated calculations and inactive branches.
  Cache/simplify equivalent work; preserve the selected normals, pigment and detail.
- Measure close ground POM/detail, reflections, water, fog and custom post-process
  passes individually. Remove redundancy or offscreen work before changing quality.
- Keep 4K bark/source textures sharp. Fix residency, unused allocations and streaming
  stalls before considering any reduction in texture quality.

5. Protect visual quality for each candidate
One measured change at a time. Keep output/internal resolution, exposure controls,
lighting, fog, wind settings, geometry populations and source textures fixed unless
the experiment specifically targets an equivalent implementation of one of them.
Capture the same local and valley views, colour and greyscale thumbnails, plus
real-time motion for shimmer, LOD popping, shadow instability and wind continuity.
Inspect both steady images and transition moments. Revert a visual regression;
do not compensate for lost detail with sharpening, stronger fog or a new grade.
Do not lower TSR resolution, thin visible grass/trees, reduce visible draw distance,
disable wind/shadows, blur textures, or simplify silhouettes to manufacture a pass.
If the hardware budget remains out of reach after equivalent-work improvements,
report that honestly with measured options instead of silently degrading the scene.

6. Integrate and validate sustained results
After individual wins pass, combine them in a recoverable candidate and remeasure;
savings need not add up. Repeat every route segment and the continuous drive under
matched power/thermal conditions. Run a longer foreground driving soak to expose
streaming/VRAM growth, accumulating simulation state and thermal throttling.
Validate mud, water, contact persistence, vehicle behavior and reset/reload. Compare
normal launch with the benchmark configuration; no private preview-only settings.

Deliverables
- Baseline hardware/settings/route manifest and raw timings/traces.
- Ranked bottleneck list with per-segment costs and prioritized experiments.
- Per-change measured gain, visual evidence, behavior checks and rollback point.
- Final per-segment p95/p99/hitch report and real foreground 60fps pacing check.
- Explicit remaining limitations and a reproducible normal launch.
Source/config/documentation only in Git; no purchased assets or credentials.
No 60fps success is declared until this evidence exists.
