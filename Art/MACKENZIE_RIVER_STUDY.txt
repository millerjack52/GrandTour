Scope
A 36 m detailed reach in a larger context bed, built as one continuous static
collision surface, with a shallow channel and tapered gravel island. Packed
pebble material uses matched colour/normal/height/roughness/AO maps; dry, damp,
and submerged states share the same coordinates. POM depth 3 cm fades with
distance and grazing angle. Scan-driven mesh relief is bounded to +/-0.9 cm.
Beyond 12-28 m the shingle transitions to three hashed texture offsets/rotations
blended over a warped triangular lattice. Colour, normal, roughness and AO share
coordinates and weights. Normal vectors rotate back into world space, and
variance compensation keeps blended patches from losing contrast. This retains
stone scale while breaking the regular 1.35 m repeat and common orientation.
Broad sediment colour variation continues beyond the scattered stone deposits.
The bed now grades through 5/20/50/100 cm spacing along the river, with relief
filtered to its local sampling interval outside the original fine reach. This
replaces the abrupt 20 cm to 5 m jump and aliased distant facets. Water clipping
samples the exported bed triangles, including the filtered relief.
Outside the detailed 36x20 m ford, relief blends over 15 m into nonperiodic
terrain noise. The previous sum of sine waves became regular corrugations in
wider views even after the texture repeat was broken up. The fine ford remains
unchanged; the noise bands are filtered against the outer mesh's sample spacing.
The bed's Nanite fallback retains source triangles for complex collision. The
automatic fallback flattened this small reach within the large context mesh:
the first runtime survey found errors up to 112.36 cm below the authored bed,
which made the car read excessive water depth and flood. collision-review.json
records the latest 15-point survey; collision-repair-evidence.json preserves the
before/after repair. All repaired points matched within 0.75 cm, including the
scan relief. The visible Nanite mesh is unchanged. Full fallback collision is
appropriate for this isolated study; a production landscape remains the target.
Partly buried cobbles have eight reusable shapes and patchy, size-varied placement.
Quiet silt pockets suppress cobbles. Sparse tussocks and five wood fragments
complete the margins. The revised margins reuse the approved brown_mud_02 and
mud_cracked_dry_03 scans and BenmoreScannedMud shading: 260/180 cm repeats and
2.5 cm POM. This restores material continuity; it does not add the forest
study's churned geometry, ruts or soil simulation.

The water is cloned from the material actually assigned to Benmore_RS_WaterDomain
in the saved Gaea Benmore map (currently WaterSurface/M_GC_DomainRiver), with its
installed M_FW_Precombine. Local flow/bank coordinates, texture bindings and the
static coverage mask are adapted. The study's short data bake also receives a
6 m edge blend so its current-driven waves do not end on a straight boundary.
The original map and materials are not saved.
benmore-water-reference.json records the exact source and original shader text.
A 25 cm local water mesh grades to 50 cm through +/-180 m and 100 cm farther out,
preserving wave/shore geometry beyond the close-up area instead of jumping to 5 m.
The native GTWaterSheet uses a study-owned 25 cm depth/current bake over 80x40 m;
its moving simulation window and ambient-wave settings match Benmore. The far
context is visual only. Dry/wet coverage and live material bindings are checked.

No Benmore map or physics edits. Small stones are decorative, without collision.
The static bed has no authored physical surface zones and currently falls back
to the vehicle's tarmac behaviour. Mud does not sink or rut. Proper loose-gravel,
wet-ground and finite-depth mud behaviour requires the existing terrain surface
system, not a cosmetic material change. Water depth/current now reach the existing
water subsystem; the optional drive review checks actual tyre/hull/spray response.
Hills are simple context geometry; this is not a surveyed Mackenzie location.
Performance has not been benchmarked (owner measures performance).
The distance revision saved the level and all seven captures with no final
material or Python errors. Its 15-point collision survey still matched within
0.75 cm, and dry/wet queries and live water bindings passed. The outer source
mesh is denser: 3,067,480 bed triangles and 114,844 water triangles. This is an
art-study correction, not a performance-validated production topology.

References and provenance
Art/Export/MackenzieRiverConcept/concept.html links the approved generated concept
and the original Ohau, Ahuriri and Godley reference photographs. The Ohau source
documents didymo: only water clarity and stone scale guided this study, not its
thick algal mat. Regional photos are not imported textures.

The original River Small Rocks scan read too soil-heavy in the first scene.
Final shingle uses Poly Haven's Ganges River Pebbles by Amal Kumar, CC0:
https://polyhaven.com/a/ganges_river_pebbles
It is a material source, not New Zealand geology documentation. The shader
reduces warm/purple saturation to fit the reference palette. Source files remain
unmodified; download_mackenzie_river_textures.ps1 verifies published MD5 hashes.
Art/Textures/MackenzieRiver/source-manifest.json stores exact URLs and hashes.

Rebuild
1. powershell -NoProfile -File Art/download_mackenzie_river_textures.ps1
2. Run Blender 5.1 hidden/background with --python Art/Blender/build_mackenzie_river_study.py
3. As its OWN command: powershell -NoProfile -File Art/wait_unreal_free.ps1 -MaxMinutes 0
4. Only after step 3 returns 0:
   powershell -NoProfile -File Art/build_mackenzie_river_study.ps1
The Unreal runner reserves Local\GrandTour-BenmoreProfiling, saves this dedicated
level, captures seven views and exits. Existing level copies go to Saved/MackenzieRiverStudy.
The runner uses -GTLook=0 -GTLookSky=0 -GTLookHaze=0 for neutral material inspection.
Use those launch flags for matching Play captures, or set gt.Look.Post, gt.Look.Sky
and gt.Look.Haze to 0 in the editor console before Play. The saved study exposure
has priority 500 so the ordinary game exposure preset cannot wash out daylight.

Optional functional water crossing (run the guard again as its own command):
   powershell -NoProfile -File Art/build_mackenzie_river_study.ps1 -DriveReview
It uses a native Range Rover in Play and drives it across with normal
throttle/brake/steering inputs at a fixed simulation timestep. It records contact,
surface tag, water depth, forces and spray in drive-report.json. No level is saved.
It checks the bed collider in Play first; an old simplified fallback is backed up
and repaired with Play stopped before repeating the check and driving.
This is a water connection check, not approval of handling or a performance test.
The final crossing completed with 54 samples at a fixed 1/60 s timestep, dry
approach/exit, active tyre and hull water forces and splash/bow spray. Maximum
sampled wheel water depth was 0.62 m (including dynamic water). All wheels still
reported Surface.Tarmac. The scene is not a finished gravel/mud driving section.
An earlier capture harness run exhausted VRAM; the final run drains GPU work
each frame, including background rain captures when viewport rendering is off.
The successful final crossing still returned the known shutdown access violation
after saving its report/capture; drive-run-status.json separates those results.

The geometry .blend, FBX files, imported assets and captures are generated local
artifacts. The source scripts plus this document reproduce the study using the
existing approved mud assets and the installed Benmore water assets.
